SI units & fundamental constants

Thermodynamics. Temperature \(T\) in kelvins \(\mathrm{K}\); internal energy \(U\), microstate energy \(E_i\), heat \(Q\), work \(W\), free energies and chemical potential \(\mu\) in joules \(\mathrm{J}\) (chemical potential is energy per particle); entropy \(S\) and heat capacity \(C_V,C_P\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\); pressure \(P\) in pascals \(\mathrm{Pa}=\mathrm{J/m^3}\); volume \(V\) in \(\mathrm{m^3}\).

Statistical quantities. Particle count \(N\), multiplicity \(\Omega\), degeneracy \(g\), probability \(p_i\), occupation \(\bar n_i\), and properly normalized partition functions \(Z,\Xi\) are dimensionless; \(\beta=1/(k_BT)\) has units \(\mathrm{J^{-1}}\). Energy variance has units \(\mathrm{J^2}\).

Exact SI constants.

\[\boxed{k_B=1.380649\times10^{-23}\ \mathrm{J/K}},\qquad \boxed{h=6.62607015\times10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}}\]

\(k_B\) (Boltzmann's constant) and \(h\) (Planck's constant) have exact defined SI values. The reduced Planck constant is \(\hbar=h/(2\pi)\approx1.054571817\times10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}\).

Quantum and gases. Mass \(m\) in \(\mathrm{kg}\); length \(L\), thermal wavelength \(\lambda_T\) in \(\mathrm{m}\); angular frequency \(\omega\) in \(\mathrm{rad/s}\); energy gap \(\Delta\), single-particle level \(\epsilon\) in \(\mathrm{J}\); speed \(v\) in \(\mathrm{m/s}\); number density \(n=N/V_d\) in \(\mathrm{m^{-d}}\) for \(d\) spatial dimensions.

Common distinctions. \(T\) in formulas for temperature is kelvins \(\mathrm{K}\); magnetic flux density \(B\) is in tesla \(\mathrm{T}\). A magnetic moment \(\mu_{\rm mag}\) has units \(\mathrm{J/T}\), different from chemical potential \(\mu\) in \(\mathrm{J}\). Logarithm/exponential arguments and quantities such as \(k_BT/(\hbar\omega)\) must be dimensionless.

PRACTICE SET · MAXWELL–BOLTZMANN

Maxwell–Boltzmann

SI units: Mass \(m\) in \(\mathrm{kg}\), volume \(V\) in \(\mathrm{m^3}\), temperature \(T\) in \(\mathrm{K}\), velocity \(v\) in \(\mathrm{m/s}\), energy \(U\) in \(\mathrm{J}\).

Classical ideal gas and the Maxwell speed distribution

Before solving: classical Maxwell–Boltzmann statistics

Statement. A nonrelativistic classical ideal gas in equilibrium has Gaussian velocity components. The three-dimensional speed law also counts the \(4\pi v^2\) velocity-space shell. Equipartition assigns \(k_BT/2\) to each independent quadratic kinetic term.

Why. High speeds are exponentially suppressed, while the number of velocity directions grows with speed. This produces a nonzero most probable speed and \(U=3Nk_BT/2\) for a 3D monatomic ideal gas.

When valid. Dilute, nondegenerate particles with negligible interactions; quantized or additional internal degrees of freedom change these results. See equipartition.

PROBLEM 01 · MAXWELL–BOLTZMANN

1. Classical ideal gas from phase-space integration

QUESTION

Consider \(N\) identical, noninteracting classical particles of mass \(m\) in a 3D box of volume \(V\), at temperature \(T\).

(a) Derive the single-particle partition function using phase-space integration. (b) Construct \(Z_N\). (c) Derive \(U\), \(P\), and \(C_V\). (d) Verify \(U\) by equipartition.

Show complete step-by-step solution

(a) Single-particle phase-space sum

The Hamiltonian contains only three quadratic kinetic terms:

\[ H=\frac{p_x^2+p_y^2+p_z^2}{2m},\qquad z_1=\frac{1}{h^3}\int_Vd^3r\int_{\mathbb R^3}d^3p\,e^{-\beta H}. \]

The spatial integral is \(V\); the momentum integrals separate. With \(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-ap^2}dp=\sqrt{\pi/a}\),

\[ z_1=\frac{V}{h^3}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2\pi m}{\beta}}\right)^3 =V\left(\frac{2\pi mk_BT}{h^2}\right)^{3/2}. \]

Define \(\lambda_T=h/\sqrt{2\pi mk_BT}\), giving

\[\boxed{z_1=\frac{V}{\lambda_T^3}}.\]

(b) Canonical partition function

For identical particles in the dilute Maxwell–Boltzmann regime, correct the independent-particle product by \(N!\):

\[\boxed{Z_N=\frac{z_1^N}{N!}=\frac1{N!}\left(\frac{V}{\lambda_T^3}\right)^N}.\]

The assumption is \(N\lambda_T^3/V\ll1\), so quantum degeneracy is negligible.

(c) Thermodynamics by differentiation

\[ \ln Z_N=N\ln V-3N\ln\lambda_T-\ln N!, \qquad \lambda_T\propto\beta^{1/2}. \] \[ U=-\frac{\partial\ln Z_N}{\partial\beta}=\frac{3N}{2\beta} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\boxed{U=\frac32Nk_BT}. \] \[ P=k_BT\left(\frac{\partial\ln Z_N}{\partial V}\right)_{T,N} =\frac{Nk_BT}{V} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\boxed{PV=Nk_BT}. \] \[\boxed{C_V=\left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial T}\right)_V=\frac32Nk_B}.\]

(d) Equipartition check

Each of \(p_x^2/(2m)\), \(p_y^2/(2m)\), and \(p_z^2/(2m)\) contributes \(k_BT/2\). Thus \(\langle E\rangle_{\rm particle}=3k_BT/2\), and \(N\) particles give \(U=3Nk_BT/2\), matching the partition-function derivation.

Answer unit check: The partition functions \(q_1,Z_N\) are dimensionless, \(U\) is in \(\mathrm{J}\), \(P\) in \(\mathrm{Pa}\), and \(C_V\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\).

PROBLEM 02 · MAXWELL–BOLTZMANN

2. Most probable, mean, and RMS speeds

QUESTION

The Maxwell speed distribution in three dimensions is

\[ f(v)=4\pi\left(\frac{m}{2\pi k_BT}\right)^{3/2}v^2 e^{-mv^2/(2k_BT)},\qquad v\ge0. \]

(a) Derive \(v_{\rm mp}\). (b) Find \(v_{\rm rms}\) from equipartition. (c) Derive \(\langle v\rangle\) by integration. (d) Compare the three speeds.

Show complete step-by-step solution

(a) Most probable speed

Set \(a=m/(2k_BT)\). The \(v\)-dependent factor is \(v^2e^{-av^2}\). Differentiate:

\[ \frac{d}{dv}(v^2e^{-av^2}) =2ve^{-av^2}(1-av^2)=0. \]

The interior maximum is at \(av^2=1\), so

\[\boxed{v_{\rm mp}=\sqrt{\frac{2k_BT}{m}}}.\]

(b) Root-mean-square speed

There are three quadratic kinetic energies, so the equipartition theorem yields

\[ \left\langle\frac12mv^2\right\rangle=\frac32k_BT \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \langle v^2\rangle=\frac{3k_BT}{m}. \] \[\boxed{v_{\rm rms}=\sqrt{\frac{3k_BT}{m}}}.\]

(c) Mean speed

\[ \langle v\rangle=\int_0^\infty vf(v)\,dv =4\pi\left(\frac{m}{2\pi k_BT}\right)^{3/2} \int_0^\infty v^3e^{-av^2}dv. \]

Substitute \(u=av^2\); then \(v^3dv=(u/(2a^2))du\) and \(\int_0^\infty ue^{-u}du=1\):

\[ \int_0^\infty v^3e^{-av^2}dv=\frac1{2a^2}, \qquad \boxed{\langle v\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{8k_BT}{\pi m}}}. \]

(d) Interpretation

\[\boxed{v_{\rm mp}<\langle v\rangle<v_{\rm rms}}.\]

The speed distribution is asymmetric; its mode, arithmetic mean, and RMS value therefore differ. Equipartition alone determines \(\langle v^2\rangle\), not \(\langle v\rangle\).

Answer unit check: The Maxwell speed density \(f(v)\) has units \(\mathrm{s/m}\); the mode, mean, and RMS speeds have units \(\mathrm{m/s}\).

PRACTICE SET · BOSE–EINSTEIN

Bose–Einstein

SI units: Orbital energy \(\epsilon\), chemical potential \(\mu\) in \(\mathrm{J}\), occupations dimensionless and Bose critical temperature \(T_c\) in \(\mathrm{K}\).

Occupation sums and Bose–Einstein condensation

Before solving: Bose–Einstein occupation

Statement. Bosons can share a single-particle orbital. In a noninteracting equilibrium gas, their average occupancy is \(\bar n_{\rm BE}=1/[e^{\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}-1]\), and appropriate 3D gases can develop macroscopic ground-state occupancy.

Why. Unlike fermions, they are not excluded from occupied states.

When valid. Independent bosonic orbitals at equilibrium; condensation depends on dimension and confinement. See Bose statistics.

PROBLEM 06 · BOSE–EINSTEIN

6. Derive MB, BE, and FD occupation numbers

QUESTION

A single-particle orbital of energy \(\epsilon\) exchanges energy and particles with a reservoir at \(T,\mu\).

(a) Derive Bose–Einstein occupation. (b) Derive Fermi–Dirac occupation. (c) Obtain Maxwell–Boltzmann occupation. (d) Show the dilute classical limit.

Show complete step-by-step solution

The grand-canonical weight for occupation \(n\) is \(e^{-\beta n(\epsilon-\mu)}\). Let \(z=e^{-\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}\), so \(\partial z/\partial\mu=\beta z\). The mean number in one orbital is

\[\bar n=\frac1\beta\frac{\partial\ln\Xi}{\partial\mu}.\]

(a) Bosons: \(n=0,1,2,\ldots\)

\[ \Xi_{\rm BE}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty z^n=\frac1{1-z},\qquad z<1. \] \[ \bar n_{\rm BE}=\frac{z}{1-z} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\bar n_{\rm BE}=\frac1{e^{\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}-1}}. \]

(b) Fermions: \(n=0,1\)

\[ \Xi_{\rm FD}=1+z,\qquad \bar n_{\rm FD}=\frac{z}{1+z} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\bar n_{\rm FD}=\frac1{e^{\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}+1}}. \]

This counting applies to one spin-orbital; different spin orientations are separate orbitals.

(c) Maxwell–Boltzmann classical counting

With the classical indistinguishable-particle occupation factor \(1/n!\),

\[ \Xi_{\rm MB}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{z^n}{n!}=e^z,\qquad \boxed{\bar n_{\rm MB}=z=e^{-\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}}. \]

(d) Low-occupation limit

If \(z\ll1\), both \(z/(1-z)\) and \(z/(1+z)\) approach \(z\). Therefore,

\[\boxed{\bar n_{\rm BE}\simeq\bar n_{\rm FD}\simeq\bar n_{\rm MB}}.\]

For a homogeneous dilute gas, this corresponds to small degeneracy parameter \(n\lambda_T^3/g_s\). These expressions are average occupations of individual orbitals, not probabilities for an energy level with unspecified degeneracy.

Answer unit check: Fugacity \(z=e^{-\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}\), orbital mean occupations and \(\Xi\) are dimensionless; \(\epsilon,\mu\) are in \(\mathrm{J}\).

PROBLEM 08 · BOSE–EINSTEIN

8. Bose–Einstein condensation in a three-dimensional box

QUESTION

Consider \(N\) noninteracting spinless bosons with \(\epsilon=p^2/(2m)\) in a large three-dimensional box of volume \(V\).

(a) Find the maximum excited-state population at temperature \(T\). (b) Derive \(T_c\). (c) Find the condensate fraction below \(T_c\). State the assumptions.

Show complete step-by-step solution

(a) Excited-state population and density of states

The BE occupation is \(\bar n(\epsilon)=[e^{\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}-1]^{-1}\). Use the three-dimensional spinless density of states

\[ g(\epsilon)=\frac{V}{4\pi^2}\left(\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}\right)^{3/2}\epsilon^{1/2}. \] \[ N_{\rm ex}=\int_0^\infty\frac{g(\epsilon)} {e^{\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}-1}\,d\epsilon. \]

Set the ground-state energy reference to zero. The largest possible excited population occurs as \(\mu\to0^{-}\). Substitute \(u=\beta\epsilon\):

\[ N_{\rm ex,max}= \frac{V}{4\pi^2}\left(\frac{2mk_BT}{\hbar^2}\right)^{3/2} \int_0^\infty\frac{u^{1/2}}{e^u-1}\,du. \]

Apply \(\int_0^\infty u^{s-1}/(e^u-1)\,du=\Gamma(s)\zeta(s)\) for \(s>1\), with \(\Gamma(3/2)=\sqrt\pi/2\):

\[ \boxed{N_{\rm ex,max}=\frac{V}{\lambda_T^3}\zeta(3/2)}, \quad \lambda_T=\frac{h}{\sqrt{2\pi mk_BT}}. \]

(b) Critical temperature

At the transition all \(N\) particles just fit in the excited states, so \(N=V\zeta(3/2)/\lambda_{T_c}^3\). Solving,

\[ \boxed{T_c=\frac{2\pi\hbar^2}{mk_B} \left[\frac{N/V}{\zeta(3/2)}\right]^{2/3}}. \]

(c) Condensate fraction

Below \(T_c\), excited-state occupation scales as \(T^{3/2}\). Therefore,

\[ N_{\rm ex}=N\left(\frac{T}{T_c}\right)^{3/2}, \qquad \boxed{\frac{N_0}{N}=1-\left(\frac{T}{T_c}\right)^{3/2}}. \]

This applies to a uniform, ideal, spinless three-dimensional Bose gas in the thermodynamic limit. Separate the macroscopically occupied ground state from the continuum integral.

Answer unit check: The critical temperature \(T_c\) is in \(\mathrm{K}\), excited/condensed counts \(N_0,N_{\rm ex}\) dimensionless, and condensate fraction \(N_0/N\) dimensionless.

PRACTICE SET · FERMI–DIRAC

Fermi–Dirac

SI units: Fermi energy \(E_F\), internal energy \(U\) in \(\mathrm{J}\), Fermi momentum \(p_F\) in \(\mathrm{kg\,m/s}\), degeneracy pressure \(P\) in \(\mathrm{Pa}\).

Two-particle exclusion and the degenerate Fermi gas

Before solving: Fermi–Dirac occupation

Statement. The Pauli exclusion principle permits at most one identical fermion in each spin-orbital, so \(\bar n_{\rm FD}=1/[e^{\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}+1]\).

Why. At \(T=0\), fermions must fill orbitals successively, up to the Fermi energy, instead of all using the lowest orbital.

When valid. Independent fermionic orbitals in equilibrium; carefully count spin degeneracy. See Fermi statistics.

PROBLEM 07 · FERMI–DIRAC

7. Two particles: distinguishable, bosonic, and fermionic counting

QUESTION

Two noninteracting particles may occupy two nondegenerate single-particle orbitals of energies \(\epsilon_0=0\) and \(\epsilon_1=\Delta\).

(a) Find the two-particle canonical partition function for distinguishable particles. (b) Repeat for identical spinless bosons. (c) Repeat for identical spinless fermions. (d) Derive \(U\) in each case.

Show complete step-by-step solution

Let \(a=\beta\Delta\).

(a) Distinguishable particles

The four ordered configurations have total energies \(0,\Delta,\Delta,2\Delta\):

\[ Z_{\rm dist}=1+2e^{-a}+e^{-2a} =\boxed{(1+e^{-a})^2}. \] \[ U_{\rm dist}=-\partial_\beta\ln Z_{\rm dist} =\boxed{\frac{2\Delta}{e^a+1}}. \]

(b) Identical spinless bosons

The three occupation configurations \((n_0,n_1)=(2,0),(1,1),(0,2)\) have energies \(0,\Delta,2\Delta\). Each configuration is counted once:

\[ \boxed{Z_{\rm BE}=1+e^{-a}+e^{-2a}}, \qquad \boxed{U_{\rm BE}=\frac{\Delta e^{-a}+2\Delta e^{-2a}}{1+e^{-a}+e^{-2a}}}. \]

(c) Identical spinless fermions

Pauli exclusion forces one fermion into each orbital: \((n_0,n_1)=(1,1)\), with total energy \(\Delta\).

\[\boxed{Z_{\rm FD}=e^{-a}},\qquad \boxed{U_{\rm FD}=\Delta}.\]

(d) Zero-temperature comparison and counting caveat

As \(T\to0\), \(U_{\rm dist}\to0\), \(U_{\rm BE}\to0\), and \(U_{\rm FD}=\Delta\). Pauli exclusion raises the fermionic ground-state energy.

In a small discrete system, dividing \(Z_{\rm dist}\) by \(2!\) does not yield the exact bosonic or fermionic canonical partition function. The classical Gibbs \(1/N!\) correction works in the dilute Maxwell–Boltzmann approximation.

Answer unit check: All partition functions are dimensionless, while spacing \(\Delta\) and internal energies \(U\) are in \(\mathrm{J}\).

PROBLEM 09 · FERMI–DIRAC

9. Fermi energy and degeneracy pressure at zero temperature

QUESTION

Consider \(N\) noninteracting spin-\(\frac12\) fermions of mass \(m\), in a 3D box of volume \(V\), at \(T=0\).

(a) Derive \(p_F\). (b) Derive \(E_F\). (c) Show that \(U=\frac35NE_F\). (d) Derive the zero-temperature pressure.

Show complete step-by-step solution

(a) Filled Fermi sea

At \(T=0\), Fermi–Dirac occupation is one below \(E_F\) and zero above. Each momentum state can hold two particles due to the two spin orientations. Count states inside the momentum-space sphere of radius \(p_F\):

\[ N=2\frac{V}{h^3}\frac{4\pi p_F^3}{3} =\frac{Vp_F^3}{3\pi^2\hbar^3}. \] \[\boxed{p_F=\hbar\left(3\pi^2\frac NV\right)^{1/3}}.\]

(b) Fermi energy

\[ E_F=\frac{p_F^2}{2m} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{E_F=\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\left(3\pi^2\frac NV\right)^{2/3}}. \]

(c) Ground-state internal energy

\[ U=2\frac{V}{h^3}\int_{|\mathbf p|\le p_F}\frac{p^2}{2m}\,d^3p =\frac{4\pi V}{mh^3}\int_0^{p_F}p^4\,dp =\frac{4\pi Vp_F^5}{5mh^3}. \]

Use \(N=8\pi Vp_F^3/(3h^3)\) and \(E_F=p_F^2/(2m)\):

\[\boxed{U=\frac35NE_F}.\]

(d) Degeneracy pressure

Since \(E_F\propto(N/V)^{2/3}\), at fixed \(N\) one has \(U\propto V^{-2/3}\). Thus,

\[ P=-\left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial V}\right)_N =\frac{2U}{3V} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{P=\frac25\frac NV E_F}. \]

This nonzero \(T=0\) pressure originates in exclusion-driven occupation of high-momentum states, not thermal agitation.

Answer unit check: \([p_F]=\mathrm{kg\,m/s}\), \([E_F]=[U]=\mathrm{J}\), and \([P]=\mathrm{Pa}=\mathrm{J/m^3}\).

PRACTICE SET · PARTICLE IN A BOX

Particle in a Box

SI units: Particle mass \(m\) in \(\mathrm{kg}\), length \(L\) in \(\mathrm{m}\), quantum level \(E_n\) and \(U\) in \(\mathrm{J}\), heat capacity \(C_V\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\).

Boundary conditions and quantum-to-classical limits

Before solving: quantization in an infinite well

Statement. Hard-wall boundary conditions admit only discrete stationary wavefunctions and energies. The lowest state has a positive quantum number. At low temperature the excitation gap suppresses heat capacity; high temperature leads toward classical translational equipartition.

Why. Only wavelengths fitting the impenetrable walls are possible.

When valid. A nonrelativistic particle in an ideal hard-wall well; finite wells and other geometries change the spectrum. See box derivation.

PROBLEM 03 · PARTICLE IN A BOX

3. Quantum box spectrum and high-temperature classical limit

QUESTION

A particle of mass \(m\) occupies a one-dimensional infinite square well \(0<q<L\).

(a) Derive allowed eigenvalues and explain why \(n=0\) is forbidden. (b) Construct \(Z\). (c) Obtain the high-temperature continuum approximation. (d) Derive \(U\) and \(C_V\) and verify equipartition.

Show complete step-by-step solution

(a) Quantum states and boundary conditions

Inside the well, \(-\hbar^2\psi''/(2m)=E\psi\). Write \(\psi=A\sin(kq)+B\cos(kq)\). From \(\psi(0)=0\), \(B=0\). From \(\psi(L)=0\), \(\sin(kL)=0\), so \(k=n\pi/L\).

\[ \psi_n(q)=\sqrt{\frac2L}\sin\left(\frac{n\pi q}{L}\right), \qquad \boxed{E_n=\frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2}},\quad n=1,2,\ldots \]

For \(n=0\), the wavefunction is identically zero and cannot be normalized, so it does not describe a physical state.

(b) Canonical sum

Define \(\epsilon=\pi^2\hbar^2/(2mL^2)\). The energy is quadratic in the quantum number:

\[\boxed{Z=\sum_{n=1}^\infty e^{-\beta\epsilon n^2}}.\]

This is not an ordinary geometric series.

(c) High-temperature continuum limit

If \(k_BT\gg\epsilon\), many levels are populated, and the leading approximation is the Gaussian integral:

\[ Z\simeq\int_0^\infty e^{-\beta\epsilon n^2}\,dn =\frac12\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\beta\epsilon}}. \]

Substitute \(\epsilon\) and \(\beta=1/(k_BT)\):

\[\boxed{Z\simeq L\sqrt{\frac{mk_BT}{2\pi\hbar^2}}}.\]

(d) Internal energy and heat capacity

Here \(Z\propto\beta^{-1/2}\), so \(\ln Z=\text{constant}-\frac12\ln\beta\).

\[ U=-\frac{\partial\ln Z}{\partial\beta}=\frac{1}{2\beta}, \quad\boxed{U\simeq\frac12k_BT}, \quad\boxed{C_V\simeq\frac12k_B}. \]

The free particle has one quadratic kinetic term, \(p^2/(2m)\), and no interior position-dependent potential. Equipartition predicts the same high-temperature result. The finite box still has nonzero ground-state energy at \(T=0\).

Answer unit check: All energy eigenvalues \(E_n\) and internal energy \(U\) are in \(\mathrm{J}\); length \(L,\lambda_T\) in \(\mathrm{m}\), and \(C_V\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\).

PRACTICE SET · QUANTUM HARMONIC OSCILLATOR

Quantum Harmonic Oscillator

SI units: Oscillator frequency \(\omega\) in \(\mathrm{rad/s}\), \(\hbar\omega\) and \(U\) in \(\mathrm{J}\), heat capacity \(C_V\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\).

Partition functions, heat capacities, and identical particles

Before solving: quantum oscillators and equipartition

Statement. An ideal oscillator has energies \(\hbar\omega(n+\tfrac12)\). Its heat capacity approaches zero as \(T\to0\) but approaches \(k_B\) per Cartesian oscillator degree for \(k_BT\gg\hbar\omega\).

Why. Thermal excitation is suppressed when the quantum energy gap is too large. At high temperature the spectrum behaves almost continuously, restoring two classical quadratic contributions.

When valid. Independent harmonic modes; apply the temperature criterion to each mode in an anisotropic oscillator. See temperature limits.

PROBLEM 04 · QUANTUM HARMONIC OSCILLATOR

4. Exact oscillator thermodynamics and equipartition

QUESTION

A one-dimensional oscillator has levels \(E_n=\hbar\omega(n+\frac12)\), with \(n=0,1,2,\ldots\).

(a) Derive \(Z\). (b) Obtain Helmholtz free energy \(F\), internal energy \(U\), and heat capacity \(C_V\). (c) Verify the high-temperature equipartition limit. (d) Explain the low-temperature limit.

Show complete step-by-step solution

(a) Partition function

Let \(a=\beta\hbar\omega\). Factor out the ground-state Boltzmann factor and sum the geometric series:

\[ Z=\sum_{n=0}^\infty e^{-a(n+1/2)} =e^{-a/2}\sum_{n=0}^\infty(e^{-a})^n \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{Z=\frac{e^{-a/2}}{1-e^{-a}}}. \]

(b) Free energy, internal energy, and heat capacity

\[ \ln Z=-\frac a2-\ln(1-e^{-a}), \qquad F=-k_BT\ln Z \] \[\boxed{F=\frac12\hbar\omega+k_BT\ln(1-e^{-a})}.\]

Because \(\partial a/\partial\beta=\hbar\omega\),

\[ U=-\frac{\partial\ln Z}{\partial\beta} =\frac12\hbar\omega+\frac{\hbar\omega e^{-a}}{1-e^{-a}} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{U=\frac12\hbar\omega+\frac{\hbar\omega}{e^a-1}}. \]

Since \(da/dT=-a/T\), differentiation gives

\[\boxed{C_V=k_Ba^2\frac{e^a}{(e^a-1)^2}}.\]

(c) Classical high-temperature limit

For \(a\ll1\), use the more accurate series \(1/(e^a-1)=1/a-1/2+a/12+\cdots\). Then

\[ U=\frac12\hbar\omega+\hbar\omega\left(\frac1a-\frac12+\frac a{12}+\cdots\right) =k_BT+\frac{(\hbar\omega)^2}{12k_BT}+\cdots. \] \[\boxed{U\simeq k_BT,\qquad C_V\simeq k_B}.\]

Equipartition gives \(k_BT/2\) from momentum and \(k_BT/2\) from the quadratic potential.

(d) Low-temperature behavior

For \(a\gg1\), excitations are exponentially rare:

\[ U\simeq\frac12\hbar\omega+\hbar\omega e^{-a}, \qquad C_V\simeq k_Ba^2e^{-a}. \] \[\boxed{U\to\hbar\omega/2,\qquad C_V\to0}.\]

Zero-point energy persists, but the oscillator hardly absorbs additional thermal energy.

Answer unit check: \(Z\) and \(a=\beta\hbar\omega\) are dimensionless; \(F,U\) in \(\mathrm{J}\); \(C_V\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\).

PROBLEM 05 · QUANTUM HARMONIC OSCILLATOR

5. Anisotropic two-dimensional oscillator

QUESTION

A particle moves in a two-dimensional harmonic potential with \(\omega_x=\omega\) and \(\omega_y=2\omega\).

(a) Derive the factorized canonical partition function. (b) Find \(U\). (c) Derive exact \(C_V\). (d) Check the high-temperature limit by equipartition.

Show complete step-by-step solution

(a) Separate the quantum energies

\[ E_{n_x,n_y}=\hbar\omega(n_x+\tfrac12) +2\hbar\omega(n_y+\tfrac12),\qquad n_x,n_y=0,1,\ldots \]

Let \(a=\beta\hbar\omega\). The two sums factor because the two quantum numbers are independent:

\[ Z=e^{-3a/2} \left(\sum_{n_x=0}^\infty e^{-an_x}\right) \left(\sum_{n_y=0}^\infty e^{-2an_y}\right). \] \[\boxed{Z=\frac{e^{-3a/2}}{(1-e^{-a})(1-e^{-2a})}}.\]

(b) Internal energy

\[ \ln Z=-\frac{3a}{2}-\ln(1-e^{-a})-\ln(1-e^{-2a}). \]

Differentiate with respect to \(\beta\), using \(\partial a/\partial\beta=\hbar\omega\):

\[\boxed{U=\frac32\hbar\omega+\frac{\hbar\omega}{e^a-1}+\frac{2\hbar\omega}{e^{2a}-1}}.\]

(c) Exact heat capacity

Use \(da/dT=-a/T\). The zero-point term is independent of temperature:

\[ \boxed{C_V=k_B\left[ a^2\frac{e^a}{(e^a-1)^2} +4a^2\frac{e^{2a}}{(e^{2a}-1)^2}\right]}. \]

(d) High-temperature check

The classical Hamiltonian has four independent quadratic terms:

\[ H=\frac{p_x^2}{2m}+\frac{p_y^2}{2m} +\frac12m\omega^2q_x^2+\frac12m(2\omega)^2q_y^2. \]

Each contributes \(k_BT/2\). For \(k_BT\gg2\hbar\omega\),

\[\boxed{U\simeq2k_BT,\qquad C_V\simeq2k_B}.\]

Different frequencies affect quantum spacings and the intermediate-temperature behavior, but not the number of classical quadratic terms.

Answer unit check: Frequencies \(\omega_x,\omega_y\) in \(\mathrm{rad/s}\), internal energy \(U\) in \(\mathrm{J}\), heat capacity \(C_V\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\).

PROBLEM 10 · QUANTUM HARMONIC OSCILLATOR

10. Two identical bosons versus fermions in an oscillator

QUESTION

Two identical, noninteracting, spinless particles occupy a one-dimensional harmonic oscillator with single-particle levels \(\epsilon_n=\hbar\omega(n+\frac12)\).

(a) Find the bosonic ground-state energy. (b) Find the fermionic ground-state energy. (c) Derive each two-particle canonical partition function. (d) Obtain both internal energies and their high-temperature limits.

Show complete step-by-step solution

Define \(a=\beta\hbar\omega\).

(a) Bosonic ground state

Both bosons occupy \(n=0\), so

\[\boxed{E_{0,B}=2(\hbar\omega/2)=\hbar\omega}.\]

(b) Fermionic ground state

Pauli exclusion forces occupation of \(n=0\) and \(n=1\), so

\[\boxed{E_{0,F}=\hbar\omega/2+3\hbar\omega/2=2\hbar\omega}.\]

(c) Two-particle canonical partition functions

For bosons, choose ordered occupations \(0\le n_1\le n_2\). Parameterize \(n_1=j,n_2=j+l\), with \(j,l=0,1,\ldots\). The total energy is \(\hbar\omega(2j+l+1)\):

\[ Z_B=\sum_{j,l=0}^\infty e^{-a(2j+l+1)} =\frac{e^{-a}}{(1-e^{-2a})(1-e^{-a})}. \] \[\boxed{Z_B=\frac{e^{-a}}{(1-e^{-a})(1-e^{-2a})}}.\]

For fermions, \(0\le n_1<n_2\). Parameterize \(n_1=j,n_2=j+l+1\), giving energy \(\hbar\omega(2j+l+2)\):

\[ Z_F=\sum_{j,l=0}^\infty e^{-a(2j+l+2)} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{Z_F=\frac{e^{-2a}}{(1-e^{-a})(1-e^{-2a})}}. \]

Hence \(Z_F=e^{-a}Z_B\).

(d) Internal energies and limiting results

\[ U_B=-\partial_\beta\ln Z_B =\boxed{\hbar\omega+ \frac{\hbar\omega}{e^a-1} +\frac{2\hbar\omega}{e^{2a}-1}}. \] \[\boxed{U_F=U_B+\hbar\omega}.\]

The constant energy offset implies identical heat capacities \(C_{V,F}=C_{V,B}\). At \(T\to0\), \(U_B\to\hbar\omega\), \(U_F\to2\hbar\omega\). At \(k_BT\gg\hbar\omega\), their leading thermal energies coincide:

\[\boxed{U_B\simeq U_F\simeq2k_BT,\qquad C_V\simeq2k_B}.\]

The absolute energies retain a constant exchange-statistics offset; the high-temperature statement concerns the leading term.

Answer unit check: Ground-state and thermal energies \(U_B,U_F\) are in \(\mathrm{J}\), \(C_V\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\), and partition functions \(Z_B,Z_F\) dimensionless.
