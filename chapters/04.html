04.1 · Definitions

Energy levels, degeneracy and probability

For a system of fixed \(T,V,N\) in equilibrium with a large heat reservoir, enumerate microstates \(i\) of energy \(E_i(V,N)\). Define \(\beta=(k_BT)^{-1}\).

\[\boxed{Z(T,V,N)=\sum_i e^{-\beta E_i}},\qquad \boxed{p_i=\frac{e^{-\beta E_i}}{Z}}.\]

If an energy \(E_n\) has degeneracy \(g_n\), count its distinct microstates:

\[\boxed{Z=\sum_n g_n e^{-\beta E_n}}.\]

Meaning: lower-energy states carry larger Boltzmann weight; degeneracy increases the statistical importance of a given energy. Normalization follows from \(\sum_i p_i=1\).

Derivation · why Boltzmann weights have this form

Let the combined system and reservoir be isolated at energy \(E_{\rm tot}\). If the system occupies state \(i\), the reservoir has \(E_R=E_{\rm tot}-E_i\), so \(p_i\) is proportional to its multiplicity \(\Omega_R(E_{\rm tot}-E_i)\).

\[\ln\Omega_R(E_{\rm tot}-E_i)=\ln\Omega_R(E_{\rm tot})-E_i\left(\frac{\partial\ln\Omega_R}{\partial E_R}\right)+\cdots.\]

Using \(S_R=k_B\ln\Omega_R\) and \(1/T=(\partial S_R/\partial E_R)\), the derivative is \(1/(k_BT)=\beta\).

\[\Omega_R(E_{\rm tot}-E_i)\propto e^{-\beta E_i},\] \[\boxed{p_i=\frac{e^{-\beta E_i}}{\sum_j e^{-\beta E_j}}}.\]

The reservoir is assumed large enough for the temperature to remain effectively fixed and for higher-order terms to be negligible.

04.2 · First derivative

Derive internal energy from \(Z\)

Full derivation · \(U=-\partial_\beta\ln Z\)

By definition \(U=\langle E\rangle=\sum_i p_i E_i\), so

\[U=\frac{1}{Z}\sum_i E_i e^{-\beta E_i}.\]

Differentiate \(Z\) at fixed \(V,N\), assuming \(E_i\) has no explicit \(\beta\)-dependence:

\[\left(\frac{\partial Z}{\partial\beta}\right)_{V,N}=\sum_i(-E_i)e^{-\beta E_i}.\]

Therefore

\[-\frac1Z\left(\frac{\partial Z}{\partial\beta}\right)_{V,N} =\frac1Z\sum_iE_i e^{-\beta E_i}=U.\]

Since \(\partial_\beta\ln Z=(1/Z)\partial_\beta Z\),

\[\boxed{U=-\left(\frac{\partial\ln Z}{\partial\beta}\right)_{V,N}}.\]

Equivalently, \(\partial\beta/\partial T=-1/(k_BT^2)\), hence

\[\boxed{U=k_BT^2\left(\frac{\partial\ln Z}{\partial T}\right)_{V,N}}.\]

04.3 · Free energy and entropy

Derive \(F\) and \(S\)

From the Gibbs entropy formula to Helmholtz free energy

For canonical probabilities, Gibbs entropy is \(S=-k_B\sum_i p_i\ln p_i\). Substitute \(\ln p_i=-\beta E_i-\ln Z\):

\[S=-k_B\sum_i p_i(-\beta E_i-\ln Z).\]

Normalize \(\sum_i p_i=1\) and use \(\sum_i p_iE_i=U\):

\[S=k_B(\beta U+\ln Z).\]

Since \(F=U-TS\),

\[F=U-k_BT(\beta U+\ln Z)=U-U-k_BT\ln Z.\] \[\boxed{F=-k_BT\ln Z},\qquad\boxed{S=k_B(\ln Z+\beta U)}.\]

The familiar thermodynamic identity \(S=-(\partial F/\partial T)_{V,N}\) agrees: differentiating \(F\),

\[-\frac{\partial F}{\partial T}=k_B\ln Z+k_BT\frac{\partial\ln Z}{\partial T} =k_B\ln Z+\frac UT.\]

Natural variables and pressure

\[dF=-S\,dT-P\,dV+\mu\,dN,\] \[\boxed{P=-\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial V}\right)_{T,N} =k_BT\left(\frac{\partial\ln Z}{\partial V}\right)_{T,N}}.\]

This pressure relation assumes that the volume dependence of microscopic energies is correctly included in \(Z\).

04.4 · Second derivative

Heat capacity and energy fluctuations

Derive \(C_V\) and the variance identity

Heat capacity at fixed volume and particle number is

\[\boxed{C_V=\left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial T}\right)_{V,N}}.\]

Differentiate \(U=-\partial_\beta\ln Z\) again:

\[\frac{\partial U}{\partial\beta}=-\frac{\partial^2\ln Z}{\partial\beta^2}.\]

Compute the second derivative using \(Z'=-\sum_i E_i e^{-\beta E_i}\) and \(Z''=\sum_i E_i^2e^{-\beta E_i}\):

\[\frac{\partial^2\ln Z}{\partial\beta^2} =\frac{Z''}{Z}-\left(\frac{Z'}{Z}\right)^2 =\langle E^2\rangle-\langle E\rangle^2.\]

Write \(\mathrm{Var}(E)=\langle E^2\rangle-U^2\). Then

\[\frac{\partial U}{\partial\beta}=-\mathrm{Var}(E).\]

Use \(d\beta/dT=-1/(k_BT^2)\) and the chain rule:

\[\boxed{C_V=\frac{\mathrm{Var}(E)}{k_BT^2} =k_B\beta^2\frac{\partial^2\ln Z}{\partial\beta^2}}.\]

For an ordinary canonical system with temperature-independent energy levels, \(C_V\ge0\). This relation connects a measurable response to equilibrium fluctuations.

04.5 · Worked example

Two-level system: all five functions

A noninteracting two-level particle has energies \(0\) and \(\Delta\), each nondegenerate, with \(\Delta>0\). Set \(y=\beta\Delta\). Find \(Z,F,U,S,C_V\).

Complete solution

Sum the two Boltzmann factors:

\[\boxed{Z=1+e^{-y}}.\]

The free energy follows immediately:

\[\boxed{F=-k_BT\ln(1+e^{-y})}.\]

Differentiate with respect to \(\beta\), keeping \(\Delta\) constant:

\[\frac{\partial\ln Z}{\partial\beta} =\frac{-\Delta e^{-y}}{1+e^{-y}},\qquad \boxed{U=\frac{\Delta}{e^y+1}}.\]

Use \(S=k_B(\ln Z+\beta U)\):

\[\boxed{S=k_B\left[\ln(1+e^{-y})+\frac{y}{e^y+1}\right]}.\]

For \(C_V=dU/dT\), differentiate \(U\) with respect to \(y\):

\[\frac{dU}{dy}=-\frac{\Delta e^y}{(e^y+1)^2},\qquad \frac{dy}{dT}=-\frac yT.\] \[\boxed{C_V=k_By^2\frac{e^y}{(e^y+1)^2}}.\]

Checks: at \(T\to0\), \(U\to0\), \(S\to0\), \(C_V\to0\). At \(T\to\infty\), \(U\to\Delta/2\), \(S\to k_B\ln2\), \(C_V\to0\). The heat capacity has a finite-temperature Schottky peak.

Exam formula summary

\[\begin{aligned} Z&=\sum_i e^{-\beta E_i},&F&=-k_BT\ln Z,\\ U&=-\partial_\beta\ln Z,&S&=k_B(\ln Z+\beta U),\\ C_V&=k_B\beta^2\partial_\beta^2\ln Z,&P&=k_BT\,\partial_V\ln Z. \end{aligned}\]

Keep \(V,N\) fixed when differentiating \(Z\) with respect to temperature or \(\beta\). Do not use the same expression blindly if the energy spectrum itself explicitly depends on temperature.
