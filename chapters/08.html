SI units & fundamental constants

Thermodynamics. Temperature \(T\) in kelvins \(\mathrm{K}\); internal energy \(U\), microstate energy \(E_i\), heat \(Q\), work \(W\), free energies and chemical potential \(\mu\) in joules \(\mathrm{J}\) (chemical potential is energy per particle); entropy \(S\) and heat capacity \(C_V,C_P\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\); pressure \(P\) in pascals \(\mathrm{Pa}=\mathrm{J/m^3}\); volume \(V\) in \(\mathrm{m^3}\).

Statistical quantities. Particle count \(N\), multiplicity \(\Omega\), degeneracy \(g\), probability \(p_i\), occupation \(\bar n_i\), and properly normalized partition functions \(Z,\Xi\) are dimensionless; \(\beta=1/(k_BT)\) has units \(\mathrm{J^{-1}}\). Energy variance has units \(\mathrm{J^2}\).

Exact SI constants.

\[\boxed{k_B=1.380649\times10^{-23}\ \mathrm{J/K}},\qquad \boxed{h=6.62607015\times10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}}\]

\(k_B\) (Boltzmann's constant) and \(h\) (Planck's constant) have exact defined SI values. The reduced Planck constant is \(\hbar=h/(2\pi)\approx1.054571817\times10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}\).

Quantum and gases. Mass \(m\) in \(\mathrm{kg}\); length \(L\), thermal wavelength \(\lambda_T\) in \(\mathrm{m}\); angular frequency \(\omega\) in \(\mathrm{rad/s}\); energy gap \(\Delta\), single-particle level \(\epsilon\) in \(\mathrm{J}\); speed \(v\) in \(\mathrm{m/s}\); number density \(n=N/V_d\) in \(\mathrm{m^{-d}}\) for \(d\) spatial dimensions.

Common distinctions. \(T\) in formulas for temperature is kelvins \(\mathrm{K}\); magnetic flux density \(B\) is in tesla \(\mathrm{T}\). A magnetic moment \(\mu_{\rm mag}\) has units \(\mathrm{J/T}\), different from chemical potential \(\mu\) in \(\mathrm{J}\). Logarithm/exponential arguments and quantities such as \(k_BT/(\hbar\omega)\) must be dimensionless.

08.1 · State counting

From boundary conditions to k-space states

SI units: Wavevector \(k\) in \(\mathrm{m^{-1}}\), orbital energy \(\epsilon\) in \(\mathrm{J}\), volume \(V_d\) in \(\mathrm{m^d}\), number of states \(\mathcal N\) dimensionless, DOS \(g_d(\epsilon)\) in \(\mathrm{J^{-1}}\).

Take nonrelativistic free particles in a large \(d\)-dimensional region of measure \(V_d\), with dispersion \(\epsilon=\hbar^2k^2/(2m)\) and internal degeneracy \(g_s\). Periodic boundaries give adjacent wavevectors spaced \(2\pi/L\) along each Cartesian direction.

Count the number of states with energy below \(\epsilon\)

Each independent \(k\)-state occupies a cell of volume \((2\pi)^d/V_d\) in \(d\)-dimensional k-space. The number of states in a k-space ball of radius \(k\) is

\[{\cal N}(k)\approx g_s\frac{V_d}{(2\pi)^d}\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2+1)}k^d.\]

Using \(k=(2m\epsilon/\hbar^2)^{1/2}\),

\[{\cal N}(\epsilon)=g_s\frac{V_d}{(2\pi)^d}\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2+1)} \left(\frac{2m\epsilon}{\hbar^2}\right)^{d/2}.\]

Differentiate with respect to energy. Since \((d/2)/\Gamma(d/2+1)=1/\Gamma(d/2)\),

\[\boxed{g_d(\epsilon)=\frac{d{\cal N}}{d\epsilon} =\frac{g_sV_d}{\Gamma(d/2)} \left(\frac{m}{2\pi\hbar^2}\right)^{d/2}\epsilon^{d/2-1}}.\]

This continuum density of states is accurate when many levels lie within the energy range of interest. A finite box's exact spectrum remains discrete.

08.2 · Explicit formulas

Density of states in 1D, 2D, and 3D

SI units: All integrated-volume state densities \(g_1,g_2,g_3\) are in \(\mathrm{J^{-1}}\); \(L\) in \(\mathrm{m}\), \(A\) in \(\mathrm{m^2}\), \(V\) in \(\mathrm{m^3}\).

Insert \(d=1\), \(d=2\), \(d=3\)

Use \(\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt\pi\), \(\Gamma(1)=1\), and \(\Gamma(3/2)=\sqrt\pi/2\). The formulas below count all available states including spin multiplicity \(g_s\):

One dimension · length \(L\)

\[\boxed{g_1(\epsilon)=\frac{g_s L}{\pi\hbar}\sqrt{\frac{m}{2\epsilon}}},\qquad g_1(\epsilon)\propto\epsilon^{-1/2}.\]

Two dimensions · area \(A\)

\[\boxed{g_2(\epsilon)=\frac{g_s A m}{2\pi\hbar^2}},\qquad g_2(\epsilon)\propto\epsilon^0.\]

Three dimensions · volume \(V\)

\[\boxed{g_3(\epsilon)=\frac{g_s V}{4\pi^2} \left(\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}\right)^{3/2}\sqrt\epsilon},\qquad g_3(\epsilon)\propto\epsilon^{1/2}.\]

Concept: the energy dependence changes because the shell surface area in k-space depends on dimension. The divergence of the 1D continuum DOS near \(\epsilon=0\) is integrable; it does not imply an infinite number of zero-energy states in a finite well.

08.3 · Thermodynamic sums

Replace state sums with integrals

SI units: Occupation \(\bar n\) dimensionless, \(g_d(\epsilon)\) in \(\mathrm{J^{-1}}\); \(g_d\,d\epsilon\) is a state count and \(\epsilon g_d\,d\epsilon\) is in \(\mathrm{J}\). \(q_1=V_d/\lambda_T^d\) is dimensionless.

A large ideal gas has occupations \(\bar n(\epsilon)\); the density of states converts sums into

\[\boxed{N=\int_0^\infty g_d(\epsilon)\bar n(\epsilon)d\epsilon},\quad \boxed{U=\int_0^\infty\epsilon g_d(\epsilon)\bar n(\epsilon)d\epsilon}.\]
Derive the single-particle classical partition function from DOS

Set \(g_s=1\) for one spin state. The translational single-particle partition function is

\[q_1=\int_0^\infty g_d(\epsilon)e^{-\beta\epsilon}\,d\epsilon.\]

Insert the general \(g_d\) formula:

\[q_1=\frac{V_d}{\Gamma(d/2)}\left(\frac m{2\pi\hbar^2}\right)^{d/2} \int_0^\infty\epsilon^{d/2-1}e^{-\beta\epsilon}d\epsilon.\]

Make the substitution \(t=\beta\epsilon\), \(d\epsilon=dt/\beta\):

\[\int_0^\infty\epsilon^{d/2-1}e^{-\beta\epsilon}d\epsilon =\beta^{-d/2}\Gamma(d/2).\]

The gamma functions cancel:

\[\boxed{q_1=V_d\left(\frac{mk_BT}{2\pi\hbar^2}\right)^{d/2} =\frac{V_d}{\lambda_T^d}}.\]

For spin degeneracy \(g_s\), multiply \(q_1\) by \(g_s\).

Answer unit check: \(V_d/\lambda_T^d\) is dimensionless.

08.4 · Worked example

3D Fermi energy at zero temperature

SI units: Fermi wavevector \(k_F\) in \(\mathrm{m^{-1}}\), Fermi energy \(\epsilon_F\) in \(\mathrm{J}\), number density \(n\) in \(\mathrm{m^{-3}}\).

Integrate occupied states up to the Fermi wavevector

At \(T=0\), an ideal Fermi gas fills every available momentum state up to \(k_F\). State counting yields

\[N=g_s\frac V{(2\pi)^3}\frac{4\pi k_F^3}{3} =\frac{g_sVk_F^3}{6\pi^2}.\]

Define number density \(n=N/V\), then

\[\boxed{k_F=\left(\frac{6\pi^2 n}{g_s}\right)^{1/3}}.\]

For spin-\(\frac12\) fermions with both spin projections available, \(g_s=2\), so

\[k_F=(3\pi^2n)^{1/3},\qquad \boxed{\epsilon_F=\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}(3\pi^2n)^{2/3}}.\]

In two dimensions, the DOS is constant and \(\epsilon_F\propto n\); in one dimension, \(\epsilon_F\propto n^2\). The dimensional scaling arises directly from \({\cal N}(k)\propto k^d\).

Answer unit check: Fermi energy \(\hbar^2k_F^2/(2m)\) has units \(\mathrm{J}\).

Assumptions and boundary effects

SI units: Exact discrete energy levels are in \(\mathrm{J}\); continuum density of states has units \(\mathrm{J^{-1}}\).

Exact versus continuum: hard-wall boxes have quantized positive mode numbers \(n_j=1,2,\ldots\), while periodic boxes have signed integer wavevectors. Their boundary-level differences become small in the thermodynamic/large-quantum-number limit, yielding the same bulk density of states. In low-temperature finite systems, use discrete sums rather than continuum integrals.
