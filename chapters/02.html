\(k_B\) (Boltzmann's constant) and \(h\) (Planck's constant) have exact defined SI values. The reduced Planck constant is \(\hbar=h/(2\pi)\approx1.054571817\times10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}\).

Quantum and gases. Mass \(m\) in \(\mathrm{kg}\); length \(L\), thermal wavelength \(\lambda_T\) in \(\mathrm{m}\); angular frequency \(\omega\) in \(\mathrm{rad/s}\); energy gap \(\Delta\), single-particle level \(\epsilon\) in \(\mathrm{J}\); speed \(v\) in \(\mathrm{m/s}\); number density \(n=N/V_d\) in \(\mathrm{m^{-d}}\) for \(d\) spatial dimensions.

02.1 · Microscopic counting

Microstates and macrostates

SI units: Multiplicity \(\Omega\) and count of states are dimensionless; \(k_B\) is \(\mathrm{J/K}\), so \(S_B=k_B\ln\Omega\) has units \(\mathrm{J/K}\). Energies are in \(\mathrm{J}\).

A microstate is one allowed microscopic configuration (or quantum state); a macrostate groups microstates by macroscopic constraints such as \(E,V,N\). Multiplicity \(\Omega\) counts accessible microstates.

\[\boxed{S_B=k_B\ln\Omega}.\]

For the microcanonical ensemble (an isolated system in a narrow energy shell), the equal-a-priori model assigns \(p_i=1/\Omega\) for each accessible microstate; other states have \(p_i=0\). Equal probability is an equilibrium postulate, not a consequence of energy conservation alone.

Principle before proof: Boltzmann entropy

Statement. For equally probable accessible microstates, entropy is \(S_B=k_B\ln\Omega\). A macrostate with more compatible microscopic configurations has larger entropy. The logarithm converts a product of independent systems' multiplicities into a sum of their entropies.

Physical meaning. If subsystem A and subsystem B are independent, each arrangement of A can be paired with every arrangement of B. Therefore \(\Omega_{AB}=\Omega_A\Omega_B\), whereas extensive thermodynamic entropy is additive.

When valid. Here \(\Omega\) is a dimensionless state count under specified macroscopic constraints. In a nonuniform probability distribution, use the Gibbs expression rather than treating every state as equally likely. The simple product rule also requires independent accessible states.

Why entropy is additive for independent systems

If isolated subsystems A and B have independent accessible microstates, then \(\Omega_{AB}=\Omega_A\Omega_B\).

\[S_{AB}=k_B\ln\Omega_{AB}=k_B\ln(\Omega_A\Omega_B)\] \[\boxed{S_{AB}=S_A+S_B}.\]

Independence and weak coupling are crucial: correlations can invalidate a simple product count.

02.2 · Probabilities

Gibbs entropy and its microcanonical limit

SI units: Each probability \(p_i\) is dimensionless; \(-k_B\sum_i p_i\ln p_i\) has units \(\mathrm{J/K}\). Logarithm arguments must be dimensionless.

\[\boxed{S_G=-k_B\sum_i p_i\ln p_i},\qquad \sum_i p_i=1.\]

Principle before proof: Gibbs entropy

Statement. \(S_G=-k_B\sum_i p_i\ln p_i\) assigns entropy to an equilibrium probability distribution over microstates. It generalizes the equal-probability expression \(k_B\ln\Omega\).

Physical meaning. When probability is concentrated on just one known microstate, the uncertainty measured by this sum is zero. When probability is spread evenly over many states, the entropy is larger. If \(\Omega\) states all have probability \(1/\Omega\), Gibbs entropy reduces to Boltzmann entropy.

Conditions. The \(p_i\) must be normalized, nonnegative probabilities of clearly defined microstates. Use \(0\ln0=0\) by continuity. The statistical entropy equals the equilibrium thermodynamic entropy under the appropriate statistical-mechanical description; arbitrary nonequilibrium probabilities require additional care in interpretation.

Derive \(S_G=k_B\ln\Omega\) for equal probabilities

In an equal-probability ensemble with \(\Omega\) accessible states, \(p_i=1/\Omega\). Substitute:

\[S_G=-k_B\sum_{i=1}^{\Omega}\frac1\Omega\ln\frac1\Omega =-k_B\left(\Omega\frac1\Omega\right)(-\ln\Omega).\] \[\boxed{S_G=k_B\ln\Omega=S_B}.\]

The probability distribution matters: if microstates do not have equal probabilities, replace the simple multiplicity expression by the Gibbs sum.

02.3 · Worked example

Counting a two-state spin system

SI units: Magnetic field \(B\) in tesla \(\mathrm{T}\), moment \(\mu\) here in \(\mathrm{J/T}\), so \(\mu B\) is energy in \(\mathrm{J}\). Total magnetic moment \(M\) is \(\mathrm{J/T}\). \(N,n_\uparrow,n_\downarrow,p\) are dimensionless.

Consider \(N\) distinguishable localized spin-\(\tfrac12\) sites, each with magnetic moment \(\pm\mu\) along a fixed field \(B\). Let \(n_\uparrow+n_\downarrow=N\), and fix \(n_\uparrow\).

Before the counting: combinatorial multiplicity

Statement. Choosing \(n_\uparrow\) up-oriented spins among \(N\) distinct fixed lattice sites produces \(\binom{N}{n_\uparrow}\) microstates. Exchanging two identical up-orientations does not create a new configuration.

Physical meaning. The overwhelmingly numerous macrostates near equal up/down populations tend to dominate when there is no strong energy bias. Stirling's approximation simplifies factorials only when the relevant populations are large.

Conditions. Here the sites are distinguishable and the two spin orientations are distinguishable outcomes at each site. This counting should not be transferred unchanged to freely moving identical quantum particles, which obey Bose or Fermi statistics.

Derive the binomial multiplicity and magnetization

There are \(N!\) permutations of \(N\) sites, but exchanging two up-spins or two down-spins leaves the microstate assignment unchanged. Therefore

\[\boxed{\Omega(N,n_\uparrow)=\frac{N!}{n_\uparrow!(N-n_\uparrow)!}}.\]

For a moment parallel to the applied field, energy is \(-\mu B\); for antiparallel, \(+\mu B\). Total energy and magnetization are

\[E=-\mu B(n_\uparrow-n_\downarrow),\qquad M=\mu(n_\uparrow-n_\downarrow).\]

Set \(m=M/(N\mu)\), so \(n_\uparrow=N(1+m)/2\), \(n_\downarrow=N(1-m)/2\), giving the multiplicity for fixed magnetization.

Example: with \(N=4\), \(n_\uparrow=2\), there are \(\binom42=6\) microstates, even though the macrostate is specified by one number.

Large N: derive entropy using Stirling's approximation

Let \(p=n_\uparrow/N\). For large occupancies use \(\ln n!\approx n\ln n-n\):

\[\ln\Omega\approx N\ln N-n_\uparrow\ln n_\uparrow-n_\downarrow\ln n_\downarrow.\]

Substitute \(n_\uparrow=Np\), \(n_\downarrow=N(1-p)\), and collect the \(\ln N\) terms:

\[\ln\Omega\approx-N[p\ln p+(1-p)\ln(1-p)].\] \[\boxed{S\approx-Nk_B[p\ln p+(1-p)\ln(1-p)]}.\]

The maximum occurs at \(p=1/2\): \(S_{\max}\approx Nk_B\ln2\), when up and down spins are equally numerous. The logarithm approximation neglects subextensive finite-\(N\) terms.

Answer unit check: The binomial multiplicity and fractions \(p,m\) are dimensionless. For a magnetic moment \(\mu\,[\mathrm{J/T}]\) and magnetic field \(B\,[\mathrm{T}]\), \(E=-\mu B(n_\uparrow-n_\downarrow)\) is in \(\mathrm{J}\), while total moment \(M=\mu(n_\uparrow-n_\downarrow)\) is in \(\mathrm{J/T}\).

02.4 · Thermodynamic bridge

Temperature and equilibrium from entropy

SI units: Internal energies \(U_A,U_B\) in \(\mathrm{J}\), entropy \(S\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\), so \(\partial S/\partial U\) is \(\mathrm{K^{-1}}\), consistent with \(1/T\).

Principle before proof: thermal equilibrium as maximum entropy

Statement. Two weakly interacting systems able to exchange internal energy, but not total energy, reach equilibrium at the energy division maximizing their combined entropy. When the maximum is internal and the entropy functions are differentiable, their temperatures satisfy \(T_A=T_B\).

Physical meaning. Energy can move from one subsystem to the other, creating competing changes in the numbers of available microstates. Equilibrium is reached when a small energy transfer would no longer increase total entropy to first order.

Conditions. The composite must be isolated, the subsystems weakly coupled with additive entropy, and thermal equilibrium stable. The definition \(1/T=(\partial S/\partial U)_{V,N}\) assumes the appropriate fixed macroscopic variables. This proof does not assume equal amounts of internal energy.

Derive equality of temperatures from total multiplicity

Two weakly coupled systems A and B exchange energy while their total internal energy \(U_{\rm tot}=U_A+U_B\) remains fixed. Their combined multiplicity is \(\Omega_{\rm tot}(U_A)=\Omega_A(U_A)\Omega_B(U_{\rm tot}-U_A)\).

At equilibrium, the most probable division of internal energy maximizes \(S_{\rm tot}=S_A(U_A)+S_B(U_{\rm tot}-U_A)\):

\[0=\frac{dS_{\rm tot}}{dU_A} =\frac{\partial S_A}{\partial U_A}-\frac{\partial S_B}{\partial U_B}.\]

Define reciprocal temperature from entropy:

\[\boxed{\frac1T=\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial U}\right)_{V,N}}.\]

Hence \(1/T_A=1/T_B\), or \(T_A=T_B\), at ordinary thermal equilibrium. This shows how equal temperatures follow from microstate counting and energy conservation.

Exam caution

For a classical continuous phase space, entropy requires a properly normalized state-counting measure (with a phase-space cell such as \(h^{3N}\) and appropriate indistinguishability factors). A raw logarithm of a dimensionful phase-space volume is not thermodynamically meaningful.
