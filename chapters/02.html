02.1 · Microscopic counting

Microstates and macrostates

A microstate is one allowed microscopic configuration (or quantum state); a macrostate groups microstates by macroscopic constraints such as \(E,V,N\). Multiplicity \(\Omega\) counts accessible microstates.

\[\boxed{S_B=k_B\ln\Omega}.\]

For the microcanonical ensemble (an isolated system in a narrow energy shell), the equal-a-priori model assigns \(p_i=1/\Omega\) for each accessible microstate; other states have \(p_i=0\). Equal probability is an equilibrium postulate, not a consequence of energy conservation alone.

Why entropy is additive for independent systems

If isolated subsystems A and B have independent accessible microstates, then \(\Omega_{AB}=\Omega_A\Omega_B\).

\[S_{AB}=k_B\ln\Omega_{AB}=k_B\ln(\Omega_A\Omega_B)\] \[\boxed{S_{AB}=S_A+S_B}.\]

Independence and weak coupling are crucial: correlations can invalidate a simple product count.

02.2 · Probabilities

Gibbs entropy and its microcanonical limit

\[\boxed{S_G=-k_B\sum_i p_i\ln p_i},\qquad \sum_i p_i=1.\]
Derive \(S_G=k_B\ln\Omega\) for equal probabilities

In an equal-probability ensemble with \(\Omega\) accessible states, \(p_i=1/\Omega\). Substitute:

\[S_G=-k_B\sum_{i=1}^{\Omega}\frac1\Omega\ln\frac1\Omega =-k_B\left(\Omega\frac1\Omega\right)(-\ln\Omega).\] \[\boxed{S_G=k_B\ln\Omega=S_B}.\]

The probability distribution matters: if microstates do not have equal probabilities, replace the simple multiplicity expression by the Gibbs sum.

02.3 · Worked example

Counting a two-state spin system

Consider \(N\) distinguishable localized spin-\(\tfrac12\) sites, each with magnetic moment \(\pm\mu\) along a fixed field \(B\). Let \(n_\uparrow+n_\downarrow=N\), and fix \(n_\uparrow\).

Derive the binomial multiplicity and magnetization

There are \(N!\) permutations of \(N\) sites, but exchanging two up-spins or two down-spins leaves the microstate assignment unchanged. Therefore

\[\boxed{\Omega(N,n_\uparrow)=\frac{N!}{n_\uparrow!(N-n_\uparrow)!}}.\]

For a moment parallel to the applied field, energy is \(-\mu B\); for antiparallel, \(+\mu B\). Total energy and magnetization are

\[E=-\mu B(n_\uparrow-n_\downarrow),\qquad M=\mu(n_\uparrow-n_\downarrow).\]

Set \(m=M/(N\mu)\), so \(n_\uparrow=N(1+m)/2\), \(n_\downarrow=N(1-m)/2\), giving the multiplicity for fixed magnetization.

Example: with \(N=4\), \(n_\uparrow=2\), there are \(\binom42=6\) microstates, even though the macrostate is specified by one number.

Large N: derive entropy using Stirling's approximation

Let \(p=n_\uparrow/N\). For large occupancies use \(\ln n!\approx n\ln n-n\):

\[\ln\Omega\approx N\ln N-n_\uparrow\ln n_\uparrow-n_\downarrow\ln n_\downarrow.\]

Substitute \(n_\uparrow=Np\), \(n_\downarrow=N(1-p)\), and collect the \(\ln N\) terms:

\[\ln\Omega\approx-N[p\ln p+(1-p)\ln(1-p)].\] \[\boxed{S\approx-Nk_B[p\ln p+(1-p)\ln(1-p)]}.\]

The maximum occurs at \(p=1/2\): \(S_{\max}\approx Nk_B\ln2\), when up and down spins are equally numerous. The logarithm approximation neglects subextensive finite-\(N\) terms.

02.4 · Thermodynamic bridge

Temperature and equilibrium from entropy

Derive equality of temperatures from total multiplicity

Two weakly coupled systems A and B can exchange energy while \(E_{\rm tot}=E_A+E_B\) stays fixed. Their combined multiplicity is \(\Omega_{\rm tot}(E_A)=\Omega_A(E_A)\Omega_B(E_{\rm tot}-E_A)\).

At equilibrium, the most probable energy division maximizes \(S_{\rm tot}=S_A(E_A)+S_B(E_{\rm tot}-E_A)\):

\[0=\frac{dS_{\rm tot}}{dE_A} =\frac{\partial S_A}{\partial E_A}-\frac{\partial S_B}{\partial E_B}.\]

Define reciprocal temperature from entropy:

\[\boxed{\frac1T=\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial E}\right)_{V,N}}.\]

Hence \(1/T_A=1/T_B\), or \(T_A=T_B\), at ordinary thermal equilibrium. This shows how equal temperatures follow from microstate counting and energy conservation.

Exam caution

For a classical continuous phase space, entropy requires a properly normalized state-counting measure (with a phase-space cell such as \(h^{3N}\) and appropriate indistinguishability factors). A raw logarithm of a dimensionful phase-space volume is not thermodynamically meaningful.
