10.1 · Independent oscillators

Einstein model: partition function, \(U\), \(C_V\)

Model a solid with \(N\) atoms as \(3N\) independent identical quantum harmonic modes with a common frequency \(\omega_E\). The excitation number of each mode is \(n=0,1,2,\ldots\).

Derive Einstein heat capacity from a geometric series

A mode has \(E_n=\hbar\omega_E(n+1/2)\). Its partition function is

\[z_E=\sum_{n=0}^\infty e^{-\beta\hbar\omega_E(n+1/2)} =\frac{e^{-\beta\hbar\omega_E/2}}{1-e^{-\beta\hbar\omega_E}}.\]

For \(3N\) independent modes, \(Z_E=z_E^{3N}\). Let \(x=\beta\hbar\omega_E=\Theta_E/T\), \(\Theta_E=\hbar\omega_E/k_B\). Then

\[\ln Z_E=-\frac{3Nx}{2}-3N\ln(1-e^{-x}).\]

Differentiate with respect to \(\beta\):

\[\boxed{U_E=3N\hbar\omega_E\left(\frac12+\frac1{e^x-1}\right)}.\]

With \(dx/dT=-x/T\),

\[\boxed{C_{V,E}=3Nk_B\,x^2\frac{e^x}{(e^x-1)^2}}.\]

At \(T\ll\Theta_E\), \(C_{V,E}\approx3Nk_Bx^2e^{-x}\); at \(T\gg\Theta_E\), \(C_{V,E}\to3Nk_B\). The zero-point term \(3N\hbar\omega_E/2\) contributes to \(U_E\) but not \(C_{V,E}\).

10.2 · Continuum phonons

Debye model and the phonon density of states

Debye replaces identical-frequency Einstein modes with collective acoustic modes of different \(\omega\), approximated as linearly dispersing \(\omega=vk\) in an isotropic solid. Three polarizations are represented by an effective average velocity. Mode counting imposes a cutoff \(\omega_D\) so the total is \(3N\).

Derive \(g(\omega)=9N\omega^2/\omega_D^3\)

In three spatial dimensions, the number of k-states in a spherical shell is proportional to \(4\pi k^2\,dk\). With \(\omega\propto k\), the frequency density of states therefore scales as \(g(\omega)=A\omega^2\).

Fix \(A\) by requiring precisely \(3N\) vibrational modes:

\[3N=\int_0^{\omega_D}A\omega^2d\omega=\frac{A\omega_D^3}{3}.\]

Solve \(A=9N/\omega_D^3\):

\[\boxed{g_D(\omega)=\frac{9N\omega^2}{\omega_D^3}},\qquad 0\le\omega\le\omega_D.\]

Define Debye temperature \(\Theta_D=\hbar\omega_D/k_B\).

10.3 · Derivation

Debye internal energy and heat capacity

Convert the frequency integral to the Debye function

A phonon mode of frequency \(\omega\) has thermal mean energy \(\hbar\omega/(e^{\beta\hbar\omega}-1)\), in addition to its temperature-independent zero-point contribution. Sum the thermal portions over \(g_D(\omega)\):

\[U_{\rm th}=\int_0^{\omega_D}g_D(\omega)\frac{\hbar\omega}{e^{\beta\hbar\omega}-1}\,d\omega.\]

Insert \(g_D\) and substitute \(x=\hbar\omega/(k_BT)\), giving \(\omega=(k_BT/\hbar)x\) and \(d\omega=(k_BT/\hbar)dx\):

\[U_{\rm th}=\frac{9N\hbar}{\omega_D^3}\int_0^{\omega_D}\frac{\omega^3}{e^{\beta\hbar\omega}-1}d\omega,\] \[\boxed{U_{\rm th}=9Nk_BT\left(\frac T{\Theta_D}\right)^3 \int_0^{\Theta_D/T}\frac{x^3}{e^x-1}\,dx}.\]

Differentiate with respect to \(T\) at fixed \(V\) to obtain the heat capacity, equivalently

\[\boxed{C_{V,D}=9Nk_B\left(\frac T{\Theta_D}\right)^3 \int_0^{\Theta_D/T}\frac{x^4e^x}{(e^x-1)^2}dx}.\]

The last form follows by differentiating each mode's Bose occupation under the integral; it is equivalent to differentiating the expression for \(U_{\rm th}\) with a moving upper limit.

10.4 · Asymptotics

Derive the \(T^3\) and Dulong–Petit limits

Low-temperature Debye \(T^3\) law

If \(T\ll\Theta_D\), the upper limit \(\Theta_D/T\to\infty\). The convergent Bose integral becomes

\[\int_0^\infty\frac{x^3}{e^x-1}dx =\Gamma(4)\zeta(4)=6\frac{\pi^4}{90}=\frac{\pi^4}{15}.\]

Insert into the Debye thermal energy:

\[U_{\rm th}\approx9Nk_BT\left(\frac T{\Theta_D}\right)^3\frac{\pi^4}{15} =\frac{3\pi^4}{5}Nk_B\frac{T^4}{\Theta_D^3}.\]

Differentiate:

\[\boxed{C_{V,D}\approx\frac{12\pi^4}{5}Nk_B\left(\frac T{\Theta_D}\right)^3}.\]

Thus the low-temperature heat capacity of a 3D ideal Debye solid follows the \(T^3\) law, unlike the exponential suppression of the Einstein model.

High-temperature Dulong–Petit limit

For \(k_BT\gg\hbar\omega_D\), each mode has thermal mean energy \(\hbar\omega/(e^{\beta\hbar\omega}-1)\approx k_BT\). There are \(3N\) modes, so

\[U_{\rm th}\approx\int_0^{\omega_D}g_D(\omega)\,k_BT\,d\omega=3Nk_BT.\] \[\boxed{C_{V,D}\to3Nk_B}.\]

This is the Dulong–Petit law; it is shared by Einstein and Debye models in their classical high-temperature limits.

Dimensional extension

For acoustic phonons with linear dispersion in an idealized \(d\)-dimensional continuum, \(g_d(\omega)\propto\omega^{d-1}\). The thermal energy scales as \(U_{\rm th}\propto T^{d+1}\) at sufficiently low temperatures, so \(C_V\propto T^d\). Actual low-dimensional materials may have flexural modes, anisotropy, and finite-size effects that alter this scaling.
