SI units & fundamental constants

Thermodynamics. Temperature \(T\) in kelvins \(\mathrm{K}\); internal energy \(U\), microstate energy \(E_i\), heat \(Q\), work \(W\), free energies and chemical potential \(\mu\) in joules \(\mathrm{J}\) (chemical potential is energy per particle); entropy \(S\) and heat capacity \(C_V,C_P\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\); pressure \(P\) in pascals \(\mathrm{Pa}=\mathrm{J/m^3}\); volume \(V\) in \(\mathrm{m^3}\).

Statistical quantities. Particle count \(N\), multiplicity \(\Omega\), degeneracy \(g\), probability \(p_i\), occupation \(\bar n_i\), and properly normalized partition functions \(Z,\Xi\) are dimensionless; \(\beta=1/(k_BT)\) has units \(\mathrm{J^{-1}}\). Energy variance has units \(\mathrm{J^2}\).

Exact SI constants.

\[\boxed{k_B=1.380649\times10^{-23}\ \mathrm{J/K}},\qquad \boxed{h=6.62607015\times10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}}\]

\(k_B\) (Boltzmann's constant) and \(h\) (Planck's constant) have exact defined SI values. The reduced Planck constant is \(\hbar=h/(2\pi)\approx1.054571817\times10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}\).

Quantum and gases. Mass \(m\) in \(\mathrm{kg}\); length \(L\), thermal wavelength \(\lambda_T\) in \(\mathrm{m}\); angular frequency \(\omega\) in \(\mathrm{rad/s}\); energy gap \(\Delta\), single-particle level \(\epsilon\) in \(\mathrm{J}\); speed \(v\) in \(\mathrm{m/s}\); number density \(n=N/V_d\) in \(\mathrm{m^{-d}}\) for \(d\) spatial dimensions.

Common distinctions. \(T\) in formulas for temperature is kelvins \(\mathrm{K}\); magnetic flux density \(B\) is in tesla \(\mathrm{T}\). A magnetic moment \(\mu_{\rm mag}\) has units \(\mathrm{J/T}\), different from chemical potential \(\mu\) in \(\mathrm{J}\). Logarithm/exponential arguments and quantities such as \(k_BT/(\hbar\omega)\) must be dimensionless.

12.1 · Exam strategy

How to approach a Statistical Mechanics derivation

SI units: Partition function \(Z\) dimensionless, \(\beta\) in \(\mathrm{J^{-1}}\), \(U,F,E_i\) in \(\mathrm{J}\), \(S,C_V\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\).

For every system, start by identifying the ensemble (\(E,V,N\), \(T,V,N\), or \(T,V,\mu\)); list allowed levels and their degeneracies; construct \(Z\) or \(\Xi\); derive observables by differentiating; and check dimensions and limits. Do not invoke equipartition without confirming its classical assumptions.

Before invoking a theorem in an exam

Explain its statement in words. Equipartition concerns classical quadratic Hamiltonian terms, whereas canonical heat-capacity fluctuations concern energy variance. Do not begin by quoting an unexplained formula.

Describe its physical meaning. Identify how probability, accessible states or fluctuations produce the thermodynamic effect, and then write the appropriate mathematical expression.

Check applicability. Confirm the ensemble, held-fixed variables, classical/quantum regime, spectrum, degeneracy and any interactions or reservoir assumptions before deriving a result.

\[\boxed{ Z=\sum_i e^{-\beta E_i},\quad F=-k_BT\ln Z,\quad U=-\partial_\beta\ln Z,\quad S=k_B(\ln Z+\beta U),\quad C_V=\frac{\operatorname{Var}(E)}{k_BT^2} }\]

12.2 · Fully worked

Problem 1 · 2D anisotropic harmonic oscillator

SI units: \(\omega_x,\omega_y\) in \(\mathrm{rad/s}\); \(\hbar\omega_j,E_n,U\) in \(\mathrm{J}\), \(C_V\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\), \(Z,x_j\) dimensionless.

A single particle moves in \(V(x,y)=\tfrac12m\omega_x^2x^2+\tfrac12m\omega_y^2y^2\). Derive the energy eigenvalues, canonical partition function, \(U\), and \(C_V\). Find low- and high-\(T\) limits.

Solution · separation, geometric sums and derivatives

Separate the Hamiltonian \(H=H_x+H_y\), with independent oscillator quantum numbers \(n_x,n_y=0,1,\ldots\):

\[\boxed{E_{n_x,n_y}=\hbar\omega_x(n_x+\tfrac12)+\hbar\omega_y(n_y+\tfrac12)}.\]

The canonical sum factorizes:

\[Z=\sum_{n_x,n_y=0}^\infty e^{-\beta E_{n_x,n_y}} =\prod_{j=x,y}\left[e^{-\beta\hbar\omega_j/2}\sum_{n_j=0}^\infty e^{-\beta\hbar\omega_jn_j}\right].\]

Using the geometric series,

\[\boxed{Z=\prod_{j=x,y}\frac{e^{-\beta\hbar\omega_j/2}}{1-e^{-\beta\hbar\omega_j}}}.\]

Take logs and differentiate with respect to \(\beta\); write \(x_j=\beta\hbar\omega_j\):

\[\boxed{U=\sum_{j=x,y}\hbar\omega_j\left(\tfrac12+\frac1{e^{x_j}-1}\right)}.\]

Since \(dx_j/dT=-x_j/T\), the heat capacity is

\[\boxed{C_V=k_B\sum_{j=x,y}x_j^2\frac{e^{x_j}}{(e^{x_j}-1)^2}}.\]

Low \(T\), when \(k_BT\ll\hbar\omega_x,\hbar\omega_y\): \(U\to\hbar(\omega_x+\omega_y)/2\), \(C_V\to0\). High \(T\), when \(k_BT\gg\hbar\omega_x,\hbar\omega_y\): \(U\sim2k_BT\), \(C_V\to2k_B\).

Answer unit check: \(U,E_n\) are \(\mathrm{J}\); \(C_V\) is \(\mathrm{J/K}\); \(Z\) dimensionless.

12.3 · Fully worked

Problem 2 · Particle in a 1D infinite box

SI units: Box length \(L\) in \(\mathrm{m}\), mass \(m\) in \(\mathrm{kg}\), gap \(\Delta\), level spacing \(\epsilon\), and \(U\) in \(\mathrm{J}\), \(C_V\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\).

For a particle in \(0<x<L\), derive the first excitation gap and its leading low-temperature heat capacity. Explain the high-temperature continuum result.

Solution · gap and asymptotic partition function

Dirichlet boundary conditions give \(\psi_n(x)=\sqrt{2/L}\sin(n\pi x/L)\) with \(n=1,2,\ldots\) and \(E_n=\epsilon n^2\), \(\epsilon=\pi^2\hbar^2/(2mL^2)\).

The ground and first excited energies are \(E_1=\epsilon\) and \(E_2=4\epsilon\), so

\[\boxed{\Delta=E_2-E_1=3\epsilon=\frac{3\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2}}.\]

For \(k_BT\ll\Delta\), approximate \(Z\approx e^{-\beta\epsilon}(1+e^{-\beta\Delta})\). With \(y=\beta\Delta\),

\[U\approx\epsilon+\frac{\Delta}{e^y+1},\qquad \boxed{C_V\approx k_By^2\frac{e^y}{(e^y+1)^2}\sim k_By^2e^{-y}}.\]

At sufficiently high \(T\), approximate \(\sum_{n=1}^\infty e^{-\beta\epsilon n^2}\) by \(\int_0^\infty e^{-\beta\epsilon n^2}dn=L/\lambda_T\), giving \(U\sim\frac12 k_BT\) and \(C_V\to\frac12k_B\).

Answer unit check: \(\Delta=3\pi^2\hbar^2/(2mL^2)\) is in \(\mathrm{J}\), \(C_V\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\).

12.4 · Fully worked

Problem 3 · Derive all ideal-gas functions

SI units: Partition \(Z_N\), ratio \(V/(N\lambda_T^3)\) dimensionless, \(F,U\) in \(\mathrm{J}\), \(P\) in \(\mathrm{Pa}\), \(S,C_V\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\).

For \(N\) identical classical monatomic particles in 3D, use \(Z_N=(V/\lambda_T^3)^N/N!\). Derive the free energy, pressure, energy, entropy and heat capacity.

Solution · apply thermodynamic derivative identities

Use Stirling's approximation: \(\ln N!\approx N\ln N-N\). Thus

\[\ln Z_N\approx N\left[\ln\frac{V}{N\lambda_T^3}+1\right].\]

Apply \(F=-k_BT\ln Z_N\), \(P=k_BT\partial_V\ln Z_N\), \(U=-\partial_\beta\ln Z_N\), and \(S=k_B(\ln Z_N+\beta U)\):

\[\boxed{F=-Nk_BT\left[\ln\frac{V}{N\lambda_T^3}+1\right]},\] \[\boxed{PV=Nk_BT,\quad U=\frac32Nk_BT,\quad C_V=\frac32Nk_B},\] \[\boxed{S=Nk_B\left[\ln\frac{V}{N\lambda_T^3}+\frac52\right]}.\]

These require the dilute classical ideal-gas regime. They should not be used unchanged for degenerate fermions or Bose-condensed gases.

Answer unit check: \(PV\) has units \(\mathrm{Pa\,m^3}=\mathrm{J}\), matching \(Nk_BT\).

12.5 · Fully worked

Problem 4 · Derive boson and fermion occupations

SI units: Orbital energy \(\epsilon\), chemical potential \(\mu\) in \(\mathrm{J}\); \(\Xi,y\), and occupations \(\bar n\) dimensionless.

An independent orbital of energy \(\epsilon\) can hold unlimited bosons or at most one fermion. Show how the grand-canonical sum gives BE and FD occupations.

Solution · two different sums

Let \(y=e^{-\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}\). For bosons,

\[\Xi_{\rm B}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty y^n=\frac1{1-y},\quad \bar n_{\rm B}=y\frac{\partial\ln\Xi_{\rm B}}{\partial y} =\boxed{\frac1{e^{\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}-1}}.\]

For fermions,

\[\Xi_{\rm F}=1+y,\quad \bar n_{\rm F}=y\frac{\partial\ln\Xi_{\rm F}}{\partial y} =\boxed{\frac1{e^{\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}+1}}.\]

When \(y\ll1\), either is \(\bar n\approx y=e^{-\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}\), the dilute Maxwell–Boltzmann limit.

Answer unit check: The grand sum \(\Xi\) and all orbital occupation numbers are dimensionless.

12.6 · Fully worked

Problem 5 · Debye low-temperature heat capacity

SI units: Debye angular frequency \(\omega_D\) in \(\mathrm{rad/s}\), \(\Theta_D,T\) in \(\mathrm{K}\), \(U_{\rm th}\) in \(\mathrm{J}\), \(C_V\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\).

A 3D Debye solid has \(3N\) acoustic vibrational modes with \(g(\omega)=9N\omega^2/\omega_D^3\). Derive \(C_V\) in the low-temperature limit.

Principle before proof: Debye's \(T^3\) law

Statement. A 3D harmonic solid's low-frequency acoustic phonon density of states varies as \(\omega^2\); hence at low temperature \(U_{\rm th}\propto T^4\) and \(C_V\propto T^3\).

Interpretation and limits. Cooling leaves mostly long-wavelength sound-like modes available. This is an asymptotic Debye-model result, not an exact heat capacity for every material or temperature.

Solution · Bose integral and T³ law

Thermal energy is

\[U_{\rm th}=\int_0^{\omega_D}\frac{\hbar\omega\,g(\omega)}{e^{\beta\hbar\omega}-1}d\omega.\]

Substitute \(x=\hbar\omega/(k_BT)\), \(\Theta_D=\hbar\omega_D/k_B\):

\[U_{\rm th}=9Nk_BT\left(\frac T{\Theta_D}\right)^3\int_0^{\Theta_D/T}\frac{x^3}{e^x-1}\,dx.\]

At \(T\ll\Theta_D\), replace the upper bound with infinity. Use \(\int_0^\infty x^3/(e^x-1)\,dx=\pi^4/15\):

\[U_{\rm th}\approx\frac{3\pi^4}{5}Nk_B\,\frac{T^4}{\Theta_D^3}.\]

Differentiate with respect to temperature:

\[\boxed{C_V\approx\frac{12\pi^4}{5}Nk_B\left(\frac T{\Theta_D}\right)^3}.\]

The low-temperature lattice heat capacity follows \(T^3\) in this 3D acoustic model; it is not the exponential form of Einstein's single-frequency approximation.

Answer unit check: \(Nk_B(T/\Theta_D)^3\) is in \(\mathrm{J/K}\).

12.7 · Conceptual question

Problem 6 · When does equipartition fail?

SI units: \(\hbar\omega,k_BT,U\) in \(\mathrm{J}\), \(C_V\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\), and \(x=\hbar\omega/(k_BT)\) dimensionless.

Principle before proof: the classical equipartition theorem

Statement. Each classical quadratic energy term contributes \(k_BT/2\) in canonical equilibrium. A 1D classical harmonic oscillator has two such terms, giving \(k_BT\).

Why it can fail. A quantum oscillator has widely separated levels when \(k_BT\lesssim\hbar\omega\), so the classical rule no longer gives its exact internal energy or heat capacity.

Answer with quantitative oscillator example

Classical equipartition says each independent quadratic Hamiltonian term contributes \(k_BT/2\). For a quantum harmonic oscillator, however,

\[U=\hbar\omega\left(\frac12+\frac1{e^{\hbar\omega/(k_BT)}-1}\right).\]

At \(T\to0\), \(U\to\hbar\omega/2\), not zero, while \(C_V\to0\). At \(T\gg\hbar\omega/k_B\), \(U\to k_BT\) and \(C_V\to k_B\), as expected from the two classical quadratic terms. Therefore equipartition requires the relevant levels to be thermally dense and the classical quadratic model to apply.

Answer unit check: The oscillator energy \(U\) is in \(\mathrm{J}\) and \(C_V\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\).

Essential equation sheet

SI units: Boltzmann \(k_B\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\), \(h,\hbar\) in \(\mathrm{J\,s}\), wavelength \(\lambda_T\) in \(\mathrm{m}\), \(U,F,\mu,\epsilon\) in \(\mathrm{J}\), \(C_V\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\).

\[\begin{aligned} \beta&=(k_BT)^{-1},\\ F&=-k_BT\ln Z,\quad U=-\partial_\beta\ln Z,\\ S&=k_B(\ln Z+\beta U),\\ C_V&=k_B\beta^2\partial_\beta^2\ln Z,\\ \Omega_{\rm G}&=-k_BT\ln\Xi,\quad \langle N\rangle=k_BT\,\partial_\mu\ln\Xi,\\ \operatorname{Var}(E)&=k_BT^2C_V,\quad \operatorname{Var}(N)=k_BT\,\partial_\mu\langle N\rangle. \end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned} Z_{\rm osc}&=\frac{e^{-\beta\hbar\omega/2}}{1-e^{-\beta\hbar\omega}},\\ E_{\rm box,n}&=\frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2},\quad n\ge1,\\ \lambda_T&=\frac{h}{\sqrt{2\pi mk_BT}},\\ Z_{\rm gas}&=\frac1{N!}\left(\frac V{\lambda_T^3}\right)^N,\\ \bar n_{\rm BE/FD}&=\left[e^{\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}\mp1\right]^{-1}. \end{aligned}\]

In any derivation, state the held-fixed variables when differentiating, identify the classical or quantum regime, and check whether the result is for one particle, many noninteracting particles, or a collective mode system.
