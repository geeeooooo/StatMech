SI units & fundamental constants

Thermodynamics. Temperature \(T\) in kelvins \(\mathrm{K}\); internal energy \(U\), microstate energy \(E_i\), work \(W\), heat \(Q\), free energies \(F,G\), and chemical potential \(\mu\) in joules \(\mathrm{J}\) (chemical potential is energy per particle); entropy \(S\) and heat capacity \(C_V,C_P\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\); pressure \(P\) in pascals \(\mathrm{Pa}=\mathrm{J/m^3}\); volume \(V\) in \(\mathrm{m^3}\).

Statistical quantities. Particle count \(N\), multiplicity \(\Omega\), degeneracy \(g\), probability \(p_i\), occupation \(\bar n_i\), and properly normalized partition functions \(Z,\Xi\) are dimensionless; \(\beta=1/(k_BT)\) has units \(\mathrm{J^{-1}}\). Energy variance has units \(\mathrm{J^2}\).

Exact SI constants.

\[\boxed{k_B=1.380649\times10^{-23}\ \mathrm{J/K}},\qquad \boxed{h=6.62607015\times10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}}\]

\(k_B\) (Boltzmann's constant) and \(h\) (Planck's constant) have exact defined SI values. The reduced Planck constant is \(\hbar=h/(2\pi)\approx1.054571817\times10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}\).

Quantum and gases. Mass \(m\) in \(\mathrm{kg}\); length \(L\), thermal wavelength \(\lambda_T\) in \(\mathrm{m}\); angular frequency \(\omega\) in \(\mathrm{rad/s}\); energy gap \(\Delta\), single-particle level \(\epsilon\) in \(\mathrm{J}\); speed \(v\) in \(\mathrm{m/s}\); number density \(n=N/V_d\) in \(\mathrm{m^{-d}}\) for \(d\) spatial dimensions.

Common distinctions. \(T\) in formulas for temperature is kelvins \(\mathrm{K}\); magnetic flux density \(B\) is in tesla \(\mathrm{T}\). A magnetic moment \(\mu_{\rm mag}\) has units \(\mathrm{J/T}\), different from chemical potential \(\mu\) in \(\mathrm{J}\). Logarithm/exponential arguments and quantities such as \(k_BT/(\hbar\omega)\) must be dimensionless.

03.1 · Isolated system

Microcanonical ensemble

SI units: Internal energy \(U\), shell width \(\delta U\), and microstate energy \(E_i\) are in \(\mathrm{J}\); \(V\) in \(\mathrm{m^3}\); count \(N\), probability \(p_i\) and multiplicity \(\Omega\) dimensionless; entropy \(S\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\).

An isolated system is specified by internal energy \(U\), volume \(V\), and particle number \(N\), with a narrow energy-shell width \(\delta U\). Its accessible microstates are modeled as equiprobable. Use \(E_i\) only for the energy of an individual microstate; \(U\) denotes the thermodynamic energy shell.

\[\boxed{p_i=\begin{cases}1/\Omega(U,V,N),& E_i\in[U-\delta U/2,U+\delta U/2],\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}}\] \[\boxed{S(U,V,N)=k_B\ln\Omega(U,V,N)},\qquad \boxed{\frac1T=\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial U}\right)_{V,N}}.\]

Here \(\Omega(U,V,N)\) counts microstates in a narrow shell centered at \(U\). With a finite shell, its actual mean \(\langle E_i\rangle\) need not precisely equal the shell center; the correspondence becomes accurate in the narrow-shell thermodynamic approximation.

Why the microcanonical probability normalizes

Exactly \(\Omega\) states have nonzero probability. Summing all probabilities yields

\[\sum_i p_i=\sum_{i\,\mathrm{accessible}}\frac1\Omega=\Omega\frac1\Omega=1.\]

For an observable \(A_i\), \(\langle A\rangle=(1/\Omega)\sum_{\mathrm{accessible}}A_i\). No canonical Boltzmann factor is needed for an isolated system.

03.2 · Heat exchange

Canonical ensemble and Boltzmann probability

SI units: Microstate energy \(E_i\), reservoir energy \(U_R\) in \(\mathrm{J}\); \(\beta=(k_BT)^{-1}\) in \(\mathrm{J^{-1}}\). Thus \(\beta E_i\) is dimensionless, as is \(e^{-\beta E_i}\).

Derive the canonical distribution from a reservoir

Combine a small system in microstate \(i\), of energy \(E_i\), with a large isolated reservoir at total internal energy \(U_{\rm tot}\). The reservoir has internal energy \(U_R=U_{\rm tot}-E_i\), so the probability is proportional to \(\Omega_R(U_{\rm tot}-E_i)\).

\[\ln\Omega_R(U_{\rm tot}-E_i)\approx \ln\Omega_R(U_{\rm tot})-E_i\frac{\partial\ln\Omega_R}{\partial U_R}.\]

Use \(\partial\ln\Omega_R/\partial U_R=1/(k_BT)\equiv\beta\):

\[p_i\propto\exp(-\beta E_i).\]

Normalize:

\[\boxed{p_i=\frac{e^{-\beta E_i}}{Z}},\qquad \boxed{Z=\sum_i e^{-\beta E_i}}.\]

Constraints: \(T,V,N\) are fixed macroscopically, but the system's energy fluctuates. The spectrum may have degeneracies, which must be counted in the sum. Continue with the complete Boltzmann-distribution derivation below, including the maximum-entropy proof and a two-level example.

Thermodynamic connection: \(F=-k_BT\ln Z\), and \(U=-\partial_\beta\ln Z\). See Chapter 04 for complete derivations and heat capacity.

03.2A · Full derivation

Boltzmann distribution: where the exponential comes from

The Boltzmann distribution is the equilibrium probability of a particular microstate \(i\) of a system that can exchange energy with a large heat reservoir. The system has fixed \(V,N\), while its energy fluctuates; the reservoir fixes temperature \(T\).

\[\boxed{p_i=\frac{e^{-E_i/(k_BT)}}{\sum_j e^{-E_j/(k_BT)}} =\frac{e^{-\beta E_i}}{Z}},\qquad \boxed{\beta=\frac1{k_BT}},\] \[\boxed{Z=\sum_j e^{-\beta E_j}}.\]

Here \(U=\langle E_i\rangle=\sum_i p_i E_i\) is the mean thermodynamic internal energy; it is not generally the energy of each microstate.

SI units: \(E_i,U\) in \(\mathrm{J}\); \(T\) in \(\mathrm{K}\); \(k_B=1.380649\times10^{-23}\ \mathrm{J/K}\) (exact); \(\beta\) in \(\mathrm{J^{-1}}\). Both \(\beta E_i\), \(p_i\), and \(Z\) are dimensionless.

Derivation I · microcanonical system + reservoir (step by step)

Step 1 · Make the composite system isolated

Let a small subsystem \(S\) exchange energy with a much larger reservoir \(R\). The combined system is isolated and has fixed total internal energy \(U_{\mathrm{tot}}\), volume, and particle number. If \(S\) is in microstate \(i\), its energy is \(E_i\). Conservation gives

\[\boxed{U_R=U_{\mathrm{tot}}-E_i}.\]

The reservoir's multiplicity \(\Omega_R(U_R)\) counts the accessible microscopic reservoir states at energy \(U_R\). For one specified microstate \(i\) of the small system, the number of compatible composite microstates is \(\Omega_R(U_{\mathrm{tot}}-E_i)\).

Step 2 · Apply equal a priori probabilities

In the microcanonical description of the isolated composite, all accessible composite microstates have equal probabilities. Thus the probability of system microstate \(i\) is proportional to the reservoir's compatible multiplicity:

\[\boxed{p_i\propto\Omega_R(U_{\mathrm{tot}}-E_i)}.\]

Different subsystem energies leave different amounts of energy available to the reservoir, so they generally have different probabilities.

Step 3 · Convert multiplicity into entropy

Boltzmann's entropy formula for the reservoir is

\[S_R(U_R)=k_B\ln\Omega_R(U_R),\quad \Omega_R(U_R)=e^{S_R(U_R)/k_B}.\]

Expand the reservoir entropy to first order about a reference energy \(U_{\mathrm{tot}}\), treating \(E_i\) as small compared with the reservoir energy scale:

\[\begin{aligned} S_R(U_{\mathrm{tot}}-E_i) &\simeq S_R(U_{\mathrm{tot}}) -E_i\left(\frac{\partial S_R}{\partial U_R}\right)_{V_R,N_R}. \end{aligned}\]

Thermodynamics defines reservoir temperature by

\[\boxed{\frac1T=\left(\frac{\partial S_R}{\partial U_R}\right)_{V_R,N_R}}.\]

Therefore

\[\boxed{S_R(U_{\mathrm{tot}}-E_i)\simeq S_R(U_{\mathrm{tot}})-\frac{E_i}{T}}.\]

The approximation assumes a very large reservoir with negligible temperature change over the subsystem's accessible energy range. One may expand about any suitable reservoir reference energy; constant factors cancel upon normalization.

Step 4 · Obtain the exponential weight

Exponentiate the entropy expression:

\[\begin{aligned} \Omega_R(U_{\mathrm{tot}}-E_i) &\simeq\exp\left[\frac{S_R(U_{\mathrm{tot}})}{k_B}-\frac{E_i}{k_BT}\right]\\ &=\exp\left[\frac{S_R(U_{\mathrm{tot}})}{k_B}\right]e^{-\beta E_i}. \end{aligned}\]

The first exponential is independent of \(i\). It is therefore absorbed into the proportionality constant:

\[\boxed{p_i=C\,e^{-\beta E_i}}.\]

Step 5 · Determine the normalization constant

The system must occupy some microstate, so its probabilities sum to one:

\[\sum_i p_i=1=C\sum_i e^{-\beta E_i}.\]

Define \(Z=\sum_i e^{-\beta E_i}\). It follows that \(C=1/Z\), giving

\[\boxed{p_i=\frac{e^{-\beta E_i}}{Z}}.\]

Interpretation: the lower the microstate energy, the greater its probability at a fixed positive temperature. No claim is made that the system is always in its lowest-energy state.

Derivation II · maximize Gibbs entropy under fixed mean energy

The same canonical distribution follows from the probability distribution that maximizes the Gibbs entropy while preserving normalization and prescribed mean energy \(U\):

\[S=-k_B\sum_i p_i\ln p_i,\qquad \sum_i p_i=1,\qquad \sum_i p_iE_i=U.\]

Introduce dimensionless multiplier \(\alpha\) for normalization and multiplier \(\beta\,[\mathrm{J^{-1}}]\) for mean energy. Maximize the dimensionless functional

\[\mathcal L=-\sum_i p_i\ln p_i -\alpha\left(\sum_i p_i-1\right) -\beta\left(\sum_i p_iE_i-U\right).\]

Differentiate with respect to each \(p_i\), treating the constraints with their multipliers:

\[\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial p_i} =-(\ln p_i+1)-\alpha-\beta E_i=0.\]

Solve for \(p_i\):

\[\ln p_i=-1-\alpha-\beta E_i,\qquad p_i=e^{-1-\alpha}e^{-\beta E_i}.\]

Normalization implies \(e^{-1-\alpha}=1/Z\), with \(Z=\sum_i e^{-\beta E_i}\). Thus

\[\boxed{p_i=\frac{e^{-\beta E_i}}{Z}}.\]

To identify the multiplier, substitute this result back into the entropy:

\[\begin{aligned} S&=-k_B\sum_i p_i(-\beta E_i-\ln Z)\\ &=k_B(\ln Z+\beta U). \end{aligned}\]

For the entropy-maximizing family at fixed \(V,N\), \(\partial S/\partial U=k_B\beta\). The thermodynamic definition \((\partial S/\partial U)_{V,N}=1/T\) therefore gives

\[\boxed{\beta=\frac1{k_BT}}.\]

This is an alternative derivation of the same equilibrium formula; it does not require treating Bose–Einstein or Fermi–Dirac occupations as the starting point.

Normalized probabilities, energy ratios and degeneracy

Probability ratio of two microstates

For microstates \(i,j\), the common partition function cancels:

\[\boxed{\frac{p_i}{p_j}=e^{-\beta(E_i-E_j)}}.\]

Thus only energy differences determine relative probabilities. If every energy is shifted by the same constant \(C_0\), then \(Z'=e^{-\beta C_0}Z\) and all probabilities remain unchanged.

Probability of a degenerate energy level

If an energy \(E_n\) has \(g_n\) distinct microstates, each has probability \(e^{-\beta E_n}/Z\), but the probability that the energy level is occupied is the sum over those microstates:

\[\boxed{P(E_n)=\frac{g_n e^{-\beta E_n}}{Z}},\qquad \boxed{Z=\sum_n g_ne^{-\beta E_n}}.\]

Degeneracy \(g_n\) is a dimensionless count. Distinguish \(p_i\) (one microstate) from \(P(E_n)\) (an entire energy level).

Worked example · two energy levels

Consider a nondegenerate ground state with \(E_0=0\) and one excited state at \(E_1=\Delta\), where \(\Delta>0\) is in \(\mathrm{J}\). The partition function is

\[\boxed{Z=1+e^{-\beta\Delta}}.\]

Consequently

\[\boxed{p_0=\frac{1}{1+e^{-\beta\Delta}}},\quad \boxed{p_1=\frac{e^{-\beta\Delta}}{1+e^{-\beta\Delta}}}.\]

The ratio and internal energy are

\[\boxed{\frac{p_1}{p_0}=e^{-\Delta/(k_BT)}},\quad \boxed{U=\sum_i p_i E_i=\frac{\Delta}{e^{\beta\Delta}+1}}.\]

Check the limiting behavior: when \(k_BT\ll\Delta\), \(p_1\to0\) and \(U\to0\); when \(k_BT\gg\Delta\), \(p_1\to1/2\) and \(U\to\Delta/2\). The result for \(U\) has units of \(\mathrm{J}\) because probabilities are dimensionless.

If the excited energy has degeneracy \(g\), use \(Z=1+ge^{-\beta\Delta}\) and \(P_{\mathrm{exc}}=ge^{-\beta\Delta}/Z\) instead.

Exam distinction: Boltzmann probability versus Maxwell–Boltzmann occupation

The canonical formula \(p_i=e^{-\beta E_i}/Z\) gives the probability of one complete microstate. The dilute-gas occupation law \(\bar n_{\mathrm{MB}}(\epsilon)\approx e^{-\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}\) gives the mean occupancy of a single-particle orbital in the classical limit. They are related, but \(p_i\) and \(\bar n\) are not the same statistical quantity. For the latter, see Chapter 07: Maxwell–Boltzmann statistics.

03.3 · Heat and particle exchange

Grand canonical ensemble

SI units: Chemical potential \(\mu\) in \(\mathrm{J}\) per particle; \(\mu N\), \(E_a\), \(U\) in \(\mathrm{J}\); grand partition function \(\Xi\) and fugacity \(e^{\beta\mu}\) dimensionless; grand potential \(\Omega_{\rm G}\) in \(\mathrm{J}\).

A system exchanges both energy and particles with reservoirs at \(T,\mu\), so neither \(E\) nor \(N\) is fixed for the subsystem.

Derive \(\Xi\), the grand potential, and the mean particle number

For a many-body microstate \(a\) with energy \(E_a\) and particle number \(N_a\), the reservoir entropy expansion now includes both energy and particle transfer. Its equilibrium weight is

\[p_a\propto e^{-\beta(E_a-\mu N_a)}.\]

Normalization defines the grand partition function:

\[\boxed{\Xi=\sum_a e^{-\beta(E_a-\mu N_a)} =\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}e^{\beta\mu N}Z_N(T,V)}.\]

The probability is \(p_a=e^{-\beta(E_a-\mu N_a)}/\Xi\). Differentiate with respect to \(\mu\) at fixed \(T,V\):

\[\frac{\partial\ln\Xi}{\partial\mu}= \frac{\beta\sum_a N_a e^{-\beta(E_a-\mu N_a)}}{\Xi}=\beta\langle N\rangle.\] \[\boxed{\langle N\rangle=k_BT\left(\frac{\partial\ln\Xi}{\partial\mu}\right)_{T,V}}.\]

Grand potential is

\[\boxed{\Omega_{\rm G}=-k_BT\ln\Xi},\quad \boxed{S=k_B[\ln\Xi+\beta(U-\mu\langle N\rangle)]}.\]

Holding \(\mu,V\) fixed, \(\partial_\beta\ln\Xi=-\langle E-\mu N\rangle\), hence \(U=-\partial_\beta\ln\Xi+\mu\langle N\rangle\).

Worked example · one site with occupancy 0 or 1

A site can be empty (energy and particle number both 0) or occupied by exactly one particle (energy \(\epsilon\), particle number 1). Therefore

\[\Xi=1+e^{-\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}.\]

The probability of being occupied is its statistical weight divided by the total:

\[\boxed{\langle N\rangle=p_{\rm occ}= \frac{e^{-\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}}{1+e^{-\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}} =\frac1{e^{\beta(\epsilon-\mu)}+1}}.\]

This has the mathematical form of the Fermi–Dirac occupation of a single allowed fermionic level. The exclusion of double occupancy is essential.

Answer unit check: The mean occupancy \(\langle N\rangle\) is a dimensionless particle count; \(k_BT\,\partial_\mu\ln\Xi\) is also dimensionless because \([k_BT]=[\mu]=\mathrm{J}\).

Ensembles compared

SI units: Fixed \(U\) and fluctuating single-state energy \(E_i\) are expressed in \(\mathrm{J}\); \(T\) in \(\mathrm{K}\), \(\mu\) in \(\mathrm{J}\), \(V\) in \(\mathrm{m^3}\), and \(N\) dimensionless.

\[\begin{array}{c|c|c} \text{Ensemble}&\text{Fixed}&\text{Fluctuating}\\\hline \text{Microcanonical}&U,V,N&\text{no exchange}\\ \text{Canonical}&T,V,N&E_i\\ \text{Grand canonical}&T,V,\mu&E_i,N \end{array}\]

In this table, fluctuating \(E_i\) denotes the energy of the occupied microstate; \(U=\langle E_i\rangle\) is its equilibrium mean, not a fluctuating microstate label.

Different ensembles become equivalent for many short-range systems in the thermodynamic limit away from singularities; finite systems and long-range-interaction systems can exhibit important differences.
