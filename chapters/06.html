SI units & fundamental constants

Thermodynamics. Temperature \(T\) in kelvins \(\mathrm{K}\); internal energy \(U\), microstate energy \(E_i\), work \(W\), heat \(Q\), free energies \(F,G\), and chemical potential \(\mu\) in joules \(\mathrm{J}\) (chemical potential is energy per particle); entropy \(S\) and heat capacity \(C_V,C_P\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\); pressure \(P\) in pascals \(\mathrm{Pa}=\mathrm{J/m^3}\); volume \(V\) in \(\mathrm{m^3}\).

Statistical quantities. Particle count \(N\), multiplicity \(\Omega\), degeneracy \(g\), probability \(p_i\), occupation \(\bar n_i\), and properly normalized partition functions \(Z,\Xi\) are dimensionless; \(\beta=1/(k_BT)\) has units \(\mathrm{J^{-1}}\). Energy variance has units \(\mathrm{J^2}\).

Exact SI constants.

\[\boxed{k_B=1.380649\times10^{-23}\ \mathrm{J/K}},\qquad \boxed{h=6.62607015\times10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}}\]

\(k_B\) (Boltzmann's constant) and \(h\) (Planck's constant) have exact defined SI values. The reduced Planck constant is \(\hbar=h/(2\pi)\approx1.054571817\times10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}\).

Quantum and gases. Mass \(m\) in \(\mathrm{kg}\); length \(L\), thermal wavelength \(\lambda_T\) in \(\mathrm{m}\); angular frequency \(\omega\) in \(\mathrm{rad/s}\); energy gap \(\Delta\), single-particle level \(\epsilon\) in \(\mathrm{J}\); speed \(v\) in \(\mathrm{m/s}\); number density \(n=N/V_d\) in \(\mathrm{m^{-d}}\) for \(d\) spatial dimensions.

Common distinctions. \(T\) in formulas for temperature is kelvins \(\mathrm{K}\); magnetic flux density \(B\) is in tesla \(\mathrm{T}\). A magnetic moment \(\mu_{\rm mag}\) has units \(\mathrm{J/T}\), different from chemical potential \(\mu\) in \(\mathrm{J}\). Logarithm/exponential arguments and quantities such as \(k_BT/(\hbar\omega)\) must be dimensionless.

06.1 · Phase space

Derive the classical ideal-gas partition function

SI units: Mass \(m\) in \(\mathrm{kg}\), momentum \(p\) in \(\mathrm{kg\,m/s}\), \(\beta\) in \(\mathrm{J^{-1}}\), \(V\) in \(\mathrm{m^3}\), \(h\) in \(\mathrm{J\,s}\), thermal wavelength \(\lambda_T\) in \(\mathrm{m}\). Both \(q_1\) and \(Z_N\) are dimensionless after phase-space normalization.

For a 3D monatomic ideal gas with \(N\) identical, noninteracting particles, Hamiltonian \(H=\sum_j \mathbf p_j^2/(2m)\), and volume \(V\), the classical limit requires a sufficiently dilute, nondegenerate gas.

From the phase-space integral to \(Z_N\)

Use the classical canonical phase-space expression, with one factor \(h^{-3}\) per particle and \(1/N!\) for indistinguishable identical particles:

\[Z_N=\frac{1}{N!h^{3N}}\int d^{3N}q\,d^{3N}p\, e^{-\beta\sum_j p_j^2/(2m)}.\]

Because positions and momenta factorize, \(\int d^3q=V\) for each particle. For one momentum component:

\[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\beta p_x^2/(2m)}dp_x =\sqrt{\frac{2\pi m}{\beta}}.\]

The three components give \((2\pi m/\beta)^{3/2}\). Thus the one-particle translational partition function is

\[q_1=\frac V{h^3}(2\pi mk_BT)^{3/2}=\frac V{\lambda_T^3},\] \[\boxed{\lambda_T=\frac{h}{\sqrt{2\pi mk_BT}}},\qquad \boxed{Z_N=\frac{1}{N!}\left(\frac V{\lambda_T^3}\right)^N}.\]

In \(d\) dimensions the same calculation gives \(q_1=V_d/\lambda_T^d\), where \(V_d\) is a \(d\)-dimensional volume. The Gibbs \(N!\) factor is valid in the classical dilute limit and avoids the ideal-gas mixing paradox for identical particles.

06.2 · Observables

Derive \(F,U,P,S,C_V\) for an ideal gas

SI units: \(F,U\) in \(\mathrm{J}\), \(P\) in \(\mathrm{Pa}\), \(S,C_V,C_P\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\), \(V\) in \(\mathrm{m^3}\). The logarithm \(V/(N\lambda_T^3)\) is dimensionless.

Explicit derivatives from the classical \(Z_N\)

For \(N\gg1\), Stirling's approximation \(\ln N!\approx N\ln N-N\) yields

\[\ln Z_N=N\ln V-3N\ln\lambda_T-N\ln N+N =N\left[\ln\left(\frac V{N\lambda_T^3}\right)+1\right].\]

Helmholtz free energy:

\[\boxed{F=-Nk_BT\left[\ln\left(\frac V{N\lambda_T^3}\right)+1\right]}.\]

Since \(\lambda_T^3\propto\beta^{3/2}\), \(\partial_\beta\ln Z_N=-(3N)/(2\beta)\). Thus

\[\boxed{U=-\partial_\beta\ln Z_N=\frac32Nk_BT}.\]

Pressure from \(P=k_BT(\partial_V\ln Z_N)_{T,N}\):

\[\boxed{P=\frac{Nk_BT}{V}},\qquad \boxed{PV=Nk_BT}.\]

Entropy from \(S=k_B(\ln Z_N+\beta U)\):

\[\boxed{S=Nk_B\left[\ln\left(\frac V{N\lambda_T^3}\right)+\frac52\right]}.\]

This is the Sackur–Tetrode form for a classical ideal monatomic gas (large \(N\), correct state counting). Finally,

\[\boxed{C_V=\frac32Nk_B},\qquad\boxed{C_P=\frac52Nk_B}.\]

06.3 · Theorem

Equipartition: proof and limits

SI units: Each quadratic contribution \(\langle ax^2\rangle\) and \(k_BT/2\) is in \(\mathrm{J}\); \(a\) carries units of energy divided by the square of whatever variable \(x\) denotes.

Derive \(k_BT/2\) per quadratic Hamiltonian term

Suppose a classical canonical variable \(x\) appears in the Hamiltonian as \(H_x=ax^2\) with \(a>0\), and its domain is all real numbers. Its one-variable partition integral is

\[z_x=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\beta ax^2}\,dx=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\beta a}}.\]

Its mean energy is

\[\langle H_x\rangle=-\frac{\partial\ln z_x}{\partial\beta} =-\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\left[\frac12\ln\pi-\frac12\ln\beta-\frac12\ln a\right].\] \[\boxed{\langle ax^2\rangle=\frac1{2\beta}=\frac12k_BT}.\]

In a 3D ideal monatomic gas the terms \(p_x^2/2m\), \(p_y^2/2m\), \(p_z^2/2m\) each contribute \(k_BT/2\) per particle. A classical 1D harmonic oscillator has two quadratic contributions, kinetic and potential, totaling \(k_BT\).

Conditions: classical continuous variables, appropriate canonical measure, quadratic separable terms and convergent integrals. The theorem is not generally valid for a quantum oscillator when \(k_BT\) is comparable to or below \(\hbar\omega\).

06.4 · Distribution

Maxwell–Boltzmann velocities and speeds

SI units: Velocity \(v,v_{\mathrm{rms}},v_{\mathrm{mp}}\) in \(\mathrm{m/s}\); \(f(v_x)\) and speed density \(f_{\rm speed}(v)\) in \(\mathrm{s/m}\), so \(f_{\rm speed}(v)\,dv\) is dimensionless.

Derive the 3D speed distribution from component Gaussians

Velocity component \(v_x=p_x/m\) has normalized distribution

\[f(v_x)=\sqrt{\frac{m}{2\pi k_BT}}\,e^{-mv_x^2/(2k_BT)}.\]

Independent \(v_x,v_y,v_z\) give

\[f(\mathbf v)=\left(\frac m{2\pi k_BT}\right)^{3/2}e^{-mv^2/(2k_BT)}.\]

Count all velocity vectors in a spherical shell of radius \(v\) and thickness \(dv\), with volume element \(4\pi v^2dv\):

\[\boxed{f_{\rm speed}(v)=4\pi\left(\frac m{2\pi k_BT}\right)^{3/2}v^2e^{-mv^2/(2k_BT)}}.\]

Mean-square speed follows from equipartition: \(m\langle v^2\rangle/2=3k_BT/2\), so

\[\boxed{v_{\rm rms}=\sqrt{\frac{3k_BT}{m}}}.\]

To find the most probable speed, set \(d(\ln f_{\rm speed})/dv=0\): \(2/v-mv/(k_BT)=0\). Hence \(v_{\rm mp}=\sqrt{2k_BT/m}\). Direct integration gives \(v_{\rm mean}=\sqrt{8k_BT/(\pi m)}\).

Answer unit check: All three characteristic speeds are in \(\mathrm{m/s}\), and the Maxwell speed probability density is in \((\mathrm{m/s})^{-1}\), as required by \(\int_0^\infty f(v)\,dv=1\).

Worked comparison: ideal gases in 1D, 2D and 3D

SI units: In \(d\) dimensions, \(V_d\) is in \(\mathrm{m^d}\), \(\lambda_T\) in \(\mathrm{m}\), \(U\) in \(\mathrm{J}\), and \(C_V\) in \(\mathrm{J/K}\); dimensionality \(d\) and particle count \(N\) are dimensionless.

Show the dimension dependence of heat capacity

For a one-particle free gas with \(d\) translational coordinates, \(q_1=V_d/\lambda_T^d\). At fixed spatial volume, \(\ln Z_N=N\ln V_d-dN\ln\lambda_T-\ln N!\). Since \(\lambda_T\propto\beta^{1/2}\),

\[\frac{\partial\ln Z_N}{\partial\beta}=-\frac{dN}{2\beta}.\] \[\boxed{U=\frac d2Nk_BT},\qquad\boxed{C_V=\frac d2Nk_B}.\]

Thus \(C_V=(N/2)k_B\) in 1D, \(Nk_B\) in 2D, \(3Nk_B/2\) in 3D. This result refers to translational kinetic energy only and does not include rotational, vibrational or internal modes.
